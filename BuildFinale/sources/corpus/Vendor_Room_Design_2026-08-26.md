# THE SUPPLIER'S ROOM IN THE EVENT HUB — THE DESIGN

> **Design pass by Fable, 2026-08-26** (owner working model: Fable designs, Opus implements).
> **BINDING — port it, do not redraw it.** A delta between what ships and this document is a
> defect in the port, not a fresh design decision.
> **Drawn:** [`prototypes/vendor_room_in_the_hub_2026-08-26.html`](prototypes/vendor_room_in_the_hub_2026-08-26.html)
> **Build plan:** [`WHATS_NEXT_Vendor_Hub_And_Answers_2026-08-26.md`](WHATS_NEXT_Vendor_Hub_And_Answers_2026-08-26.md)

**The idea in one sentence:** the same link that carries the couple's celebration carries, for each
booked supplier who is signed in, a door into their own workroom — the day-of console they already
have, moved to where the day actually happens. **Nothing in the console is redrawn.** What is
designed here is the door, the chrome around the ported pieces, and the rules of when the room is
open. On screen it is called **"your desk"** (the word is the owner's to change).

---

## A · THE DOOR

**One thing, always in the same place: a slim ribbon across the very top of the celebration's page**,
visible only to a signed-in supplier whose booking is **agreed** — or a teammate specifically granted
this celebration. For everyone else the page carries no ribbon and no trace of one.

🔑 **Why the top:** the bottom edge of that page already has **five claimants**, and the room itself
wants a bottom bar. The top is free — **and a ribbon there sits above everything, including the
save-the-date film that today paints the supplier's only strip out of sight for months.**

| When | What the ribbon says |
|---|---|
| **Months before** | *"Your booking — Ana & Marco, Sat 14 Feb."* The film plays untouched underneath. **A supplier never sits through a film to get to work.** |
| **The week before** | *"Ana & Marco · Saturday — your call time 1:00 PM"* (the call time appears once the coordinator publishes one; until then the date and a countdown) |
| **The day itself** | Full width, action-coloured: *"Today — open your desk."* Condensable to a small chip in the corner |
| **The morning after** (6:00 AM → 7 days) | *"Yesterday at Ana & Marco's — look back."* |
| **Long after** | One quiet line: *"Your work here."* |

⚖ **CALLS THAT COULD HAVE GONE THE OTHER WAY, stated so they can be overruled:**
- **A supplier is not dropped into the room automatically on the day** — the same person may be
  there as a guest first.
- **The door never disappears.** It could have been dropped after a month; it stays because it costs
  nothing and **a supplier's past work is their portfolio.**

### Who gets what

- **Agreed supplier, signed in:** the ribbon, in whichever state the calendar says.
- **Asked but not yet agreed:** 🔒 **no ribbon and no room, in any state.** The room is built of
  precisely the three things withheld until yes — the venue's name, its address, the running order —
  **so there is no honest smaller room to show them, and none is built.** They keep today's strip and
  answer from their own shop.
- **Signed out, or no booking:** nothing. Someone who reaches the room's address by hand is simply
  put on the couple's page — **no message, no error, no hint that a room exists.**
- **Teammates:** an admin sees the ribbon on every one of the shop's bookings; **an agent or viewer
  sees it only on the celebrations they were specifically handed** (owner ruling 2026-08-26).
- **Cousin and florist at once:** the ribbon rides **on top of** whatever page they already get as a
  guest. Their RSVP, their seat and their camera are untouched.

## B · THE ROOM ON A PHONE — design it phone-first

Opening the room is **a full change of scene**, like the guest's live hub: the celebration page goes
away, the room fills the screen with its own top and bottom, and **nothing from the page underneath
survives into it** — the guest's bottom bar stays with the page it belongs to.

Inside is the existing console, ported piece for piece, in the order it already has. **The one thing
that changes at this address is the lettering** — the celebration's typefaces take the headings; the
console's own colours stay.

**Top chrome, always present, slim:** the celebration's name in its wedding lettering · the date · a
close (✕) on the right · the shop's own name small (tapping it leaves for their dashboard).

**Pinned beneath it: the NOW-LINE** — *"4:12 PM · Toasts — next: First dance, 4:30."* Wherever they
are in the room, they can see where the day is. Before the day it shows the countdown; with no
running order, the clock.

**The column, in ported order:** ① the banner ② the running order, updating live, **private lines
shown but marked *"don't read aloud"*** ③ the headcount ④ their kit (floor desk · song desk · script
desk · shot list · capture tool · review QR — whichever their trade carries) ⑤ the issues log ⑥ their
status posts ⑦ **"Your team" — admins only**; agents and viewers never see this piece exists.

**The bottom edge, stated exactly.** The room owns its bottom edge alone, and the rule inside the
room is **one pinned bar, ever** — nothing else may pin. Three touches, all thumb-reachable while the
other hand holds a tray:
- **Post** (left) — a status update. *That is what a person does standing up.*
- **A jump pill** (middle) naming where you are; tap and the seven pieces rise.
- **Flag** (right) — log an issue, timestamped, two taps.

🔑 **It cannot stack with what already exists because nothing else exists in this room** — the five
current claimants of that edge all belong to the page the room replaced.
**A viewer teammate gets the same room with Post and Flag absent.**

**The room shows only this celebration.** No other clients, no money, no packages.

**The clock.** The room runs on the venue's clock. The live desk is open the whole event day and
**stays open until 6:00 AM the morning after** — the same rule as the couple's own desk, for the same
reason: **receptions here run past midnight.** A multi-day celebration runs from its first day
through 6:00 AM after its last. After that the room turns to **look-back**: the running order as it
actually ran, the issues, the posts, and the capture review, all readable; posting closed.
🔒 **The capture review in particular stays open — the shutter is day-bound, looking back is not.**

## C · THE ROOM ON A LAPTOP

**Almost nothing changes, on purpose** — many suppliers will never open this on anything but a phone,
and the console is already fluid. The column widens to a comfortable reading width **and stays a
column.** The bottom bar dissolves: Post and Flag move to the top right, and the jump pill becomes a
slim always-visible list of the seven pieces down the left edge — **the room's own contents, not the
app's navigation, which never mounts here.**

---

## D · THE WAY OUT

Two exits, both always visible, nothing else:
- **✕ or the back gesture** → the celebration's page exactly where they left it, **as whoever they
  are there**. The ribbon is still at the top, so back in is one tap. **The room never traps.**
- **The shop's name** → out to their own dashboard, for money, other clients, everything that is not
  this day.

---

## E · TWO EVENTS ON ONE DAY

A caterer with a morning christening and an evening reception has two rooms at two addresses — **and
no time to hunt for links mid-service.**

One line sits under the top chrome: *"You're also at Maria's reception tonight · 6:00 PM →."* Tap and
step room to room directly. 🔒 **The line names only celebrations THIS person can enter** — the shop's
admin sees both; an agent granted only the christening **never learns the reception exists.** The
rooms never blend, and **the bridge never switches by itself.**

---

## F · WHAT THE ROOM SAYS WHEN IT IS NOT READY

**The room's shape never changes — same pieces, same order, learned once.** Each piece says something
true instead of vanishing.

- **Booked, months out, nothing filled in:** the room is the **call sheet**. The name and date. **The
  venue and its address once set — they said yes, so they are entitled.** Headcount: *"Not settled —
  120 invited so far."* Running order: *"The couple hasn't written the day's timeline yet. The moment
  they do, it appears here — the same one the coordinator runs on the day."* Kit pieces that genuinely
  work early **work early** (the photographer's shot list syncs now). Day-only pieces say so: *"The
  floor desk opens on the day."*
- **On the day, no running order published:** that piece says so plainly; everything else runs live,
  and the now-line falls back to the clock.
- **Not today:** call-sheet state with a countdown — *"43 days to go."*
- **Before the day** Post and Flag make no sense, so the bar carries one action instead:
  **"Message the couple"**, opening the conversation that already exists — not a new channel.
  ⚖ *Call that could go the other way: leave the pre-day bar empty. It is there because "everything
  is communicated there" should be true before the day too.*

**Never a blank screen; never a piece that silently isn't there.**

---

## G · THE ONE THING TO FIGHT FOR

**The room opens before the day and does not slam shut at midnight** — call sheet before, live desk
on the day, look-back after, **one door throughout.**

If the room is only a day-of screen the owner's own sentence fails: the venue address and the call
time are communicated in the weeks *before*, and confirming a shot landed happens the morning
*after*. 🔑 **A day-only room recreates exactly the midnight-door mistake this product has already
paid once to learn**, and the fix would be demanded within a month of the first real booking.
Everything else here — the ribbon, the thumb bar, the bridge between two events — trades before this.

---

## H · WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT DESIGNED

- **The seven pieces themselves.** Ported untouched. **Redrawing them here would be the paid-twice
  mistake.**
- **Chat in the room.** The couple–supplier conversation already exists and the floor desk carries
  the live asks; **a third channel would split one conversation across three places.** The room links
  to the thread, it does not grow one.
- **Anything guest-facing.** The couple's page does not change by a line. **A supplier posting TO
  guests is a different product with consent questions attached.**
- **Money.** No fees, invoices or payments. **The room is the day; the ledger is the dashboard.**
- **Buzzing the phone** when the running order advances — during a wedding. Taste and quiet hours,
  the owner's call. **The plumbing already exists.**
- **Moving the asked-supplier's yes/no into the hub.** Considered and rejected: answering honestly
  needs their pricing and package context, which lives in the shop.
- ⚠ **Weak-signal venues.** A basement reception with one bar of signal is real and hard. **This
  design assumes a connection. Making the room survive without one is its own project, and the
  biggest risk left open.**
- **Grant management beyond the existing piece.** Per-event is the ruling and the ported piece already
  obeys it.
