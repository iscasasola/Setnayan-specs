The calls I made and why

Everything the brief did not settle, decided one way so it could be drawn. Each is reversible. The ones I would put to the owner are marked.

  1. The fields are filled from the photo, and the supplier confirms them. The owner said "they need to fill them up as well", so every field stays a real box they can type into. But making a person retype a number we have already read off their own paper is a second chance to mistype it. The drawn behaviour: type it, or send the paper and it fills in — what is checked is the value in the box either way. Ask the owner: is it acceptable that a supplier who never types anything still gets a full set of matched fields?
  2. The checks run the moment a paper lands, not when the application is sent. That is the only way a mismatch reaches the supplier while they can still fix it themselves. The shipped "send for review" button stays, as the door to the human parts (anything parked with a person, and the Google Meet). It opens when the four papers are in and their checks have run — I did not make it wait for every check to be green, because a check parked with a person is not the supplier's fault.
  3. A mismatch offers two exits, and never rewrites the profile on its own. "Use the address on my COR" writes the paper's value into the profile — the supplier chose it, nobody at Setnayan looks. "My profile is right" parks that one line with a person. I deliberately did not add a third option to edit the value freehand: a third value that matches neither source is exactly what the check exists to catch. One thing I could not settle from the corpus: on an already-verified shop the identity fields (business name, owner name, and possibly the registered address) lock and changes go through the existing correction-request door. If registered address is one of the locked eight, "Use the address on my COR" must file a correction request instead of writing directly — same button, different plumbing, and the copy should then say "asks Setnayan to update it". I drew the direct write.
  4. The Mayor's Permit is part automatic, part human — drawn honestly, per line. The owner asked whether it could be automated. What can be read confidently (the city, this year's date, the name) is checked on its own like everything else; the line that could not be read is the only one that waits. The sentence under the paper says why this one is slower, once, in plain words. It never says "manual check".
  5. The bank proof has no typed field, and says what it is for instead. The brief lists no column for it, and inventing one (an "account name" box) would be a fifth thing to type that we then compare against a screenshot of itself. Its whole job is one sentence — the name on the account is one of the two names already on the papers above — so that is what the right-hand side says. If the owner wants the account name captured as data for payouts later, that is a column decision, not a design one.
  6. The registry going down parks one check and touches nothing else. Drawn as its own state (1b) because a supplier will meet it: the three fields under the certificate read "waiting for DTI's record", not "matched" and not "problem", and the paper stays sent. Retry for a day, then a person. I chose "a day" as the retry window; it is a guess and should be whatever the retry job actually does.
  7. Nothing on the page says the QR was checked, and nothing ever should. The DTI QR is a link that anyone can print on anything. The line under the certificate says "compared with DTI's own record for this number", which is the true claim. I would keep that wording in the build and add a guard so "QR verified" never appears.
  8. The unit counted is the check — eight of them — not the paper. Five matched, one to fix, one with a person, one not sent, plus a bar. This is what makes a shop with one mismatch feel four-fifths done. The summary sentence under the bar names the two things the supplier can still do and says the rest is on us. Ask the owner: the count of eight is a design number — it changes if a column is added or if the bank name becomes data. Fine as drawn?
  9. On a verified shop the badge stays while the papers come in. Both real shops are in this state today, so the empty frame is drawn with the badge kept and no deadline. I do not know whether the owner wants a deadline, or whether the badge should ever come down for a verified shop that never sends its papers. This is his call; the banner's first sentence changes with it.
  10. The legal form is never asked. The document says whether it is DTI or SEC, and the "read as" line tells the supplier what we saw, so a wrong read is visible before it does harm. If the read cannot tell, that pair goes to a person — it does not become a question to the supplier. Honest gap: I am confident about DTI's registry because it was decoded and reached today. I am not confident an SEC registration can be checked against an SEC record automatically at all; the SEC pair may be "read the paper, compare with the profile, a person confirms" until proven otherwise. The copy is written so it is true either way.
  11. The existing "Government registration number" box is folded into pair 1. The shipped section already has a lone box for it, with a duplicate-shop warning. It is the same column as the registration number beside the certificate, so it now lives there — the anti-farming check is kept as the second half of that line's status ("no other Setnayan shop uses this number"). Two boxes for one number would be the drift the pairs exist to remove.
  12. Expiry and scope from the DTI certificate are shown, not stored. The brief names no column for them, and both are useful to the supplier and to a reviewer, so they appear as read-only lines under the paper. Whether they deserve columns — the expiry in particular, since a five-year DTI registration will lapse on real shops — is a schema decision I did not make.
  13. On the phone, the fields stay under their own paper. The obvious alternative — all four uploads, then all the fields as one long form — is the one-page-nobody-finishes the owner was warned about. The "proves ↓" strip is the seam that keeps each pair one unit when it can no longer be side by side. Mismatch values stack one above the other at that width, the paper's value still marked.
  14. One palette correction for the port. The house prototype kit's faint grey (#767A82) measures 4.31:1 on white, under the 4.5:1 floor, and it is used for the small mono labels. This file uses #6B6F77 (5.04:1). Every other pairing here was checked: action colour on white 4.76, green on its tint 5.66, amber on its tint 5.31, slate-blue on white 8.5. Gold appears only as the banner's edge, never as text.
