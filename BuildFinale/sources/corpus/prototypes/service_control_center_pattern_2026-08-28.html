Service Control Centres — the content is the page

The owner, on the rebuilt Papic page: "it doesn't look like a photo app control center. it still feels like it is a business page. with not much imagery and app feel UI." The structure shipped two days ago is right and stays — four facts, one next step, four ways in, the library, credits, set-once rows, offers last. What changes is what the page opens on: every service now leads with its own content — the library, the broadcast stage, the couple's own live page — the way every photo product in the market does. When the content doesn't exist yet, the page shows the space it will fill, honestly: an empty film roll and a countdown, never a blank.

What the market does — the owner asked for this half explicitly. Full citations in the design doc.
01 · THE GRID IS THE HOME

Google Photos · Apple Photos

Both open directly on the photo grid — no dashboard, no settings card first. Apple's 2024 redesign deleted the tabs entirely: the grid on top, photo-filled rows below, utilities last. Google demoted albums, printing and settings behind one tab and made the grid tiles bigger. If the first screen is a form, it reads as a business page.

02 · ANTICIPATION-SHAPED EMPTINESS

Dispo · Lapse · POV · Kuha

The disposable-camera rivals never show a blank pre-event page. Dispo draws the back of a real camera with a develop countdown; Lapse shows a darkroom of obscured frames you hold to reveal; Kuha's pre-event page is a designed countdown. The empty state is a roll waiting to be filled, not an apology.

03 · NUMBERS AS FILLING METERS

Eventbrite · Luma · Kuha

Consoles that feel alive show fractions filling toward a visible capacity — "128 of 200 tickets", uploads counting over a photo carousel — never raw stat rows. Luma anchors the host's manage view with the same cover image the guests see: you manage from inside the guest-facing page.

04 · STATE LIVES ON THE MEDIA

Frame.io · Dropbox

Status badges, version counts and approvals render in the corner of the thumbnail, never in a list beside it. Hovering a tile scrubs the video. Moderation attaches to the photo, configuration to the camera — the object, not a settings page.

05 · ONE DOOR FOR SETTINGS

StreamYard · Riverside · Restream

The centre of a control room is always the output the audience sees; people waiting sit in a strip below it; global settings hide behind one icon. Pre-live and live are the same screen with a colour flip — the pre-event state is a preview of the show, never a form.

06 · WHAT THIS PAGE TAKES FROM IT

Five moves, no invention

(1) the stage — the service's own content — opens the page; (2) the empty state is a film roll with a countdown; (3) the facts fuse onto the stage as its caption bar; (4) credits and per-source counts render as filling meters and on-tile chips; (5) settings stay in quiet rows below the fold, one sheet each — which the 08-25 structure already does.

One pattern · eighteen services · two lives

The shared pattern — seven slots, and a rule for every state

Measured on 2026-08-28: the 18 service surfaces share no pattern — sizes run from a redirect stub to 2,283 lines, half wear the App-Store buy layout and half wear nothing. But two shared things already ship and are kept: the App-Store detail layout is the right shape for a service the couple hasn't started (the shop window), and the 08-25 Papic structure is the right shape for one they have (the control room). This pattern names the control room's slots so all 18 fill them the same way. A service uses the slots it has content for — a small service is three slots long and that is correct, not unfinished.

S1

The stage — the service's own content, first

The obsidian Gallery-archetype panel. Papic: the library mosaic. Live Studio: the broadcast monitor. Event Hub: the couple's own page, miniature and live. Save-the-date: the film's opening frame. Pakanta: the song. Empty rule: the stage never disappears — it shows the frames the content will fill, a countdown to when filling starts, and the one act that starts it.

S2

The four facts — fused onto the stage's lower edge

The same four cells the 08-25 drawing locked, now the stage's caption bar: what's in it · ways in · still coming · the money fact. Still the first text on the page.

S3

One next step

The door-register card, unchanged: paper, 3px terracotta top edge, ONE terracotta action. The page always knows the single thing to do now.

S4

The parts — rows with their state on them

The service's moving parts as rows (Papic: four sources · Live Studio: cameras · Hub: the four stages). Source rows carry a thumb cluster — three mini-frames showing what that source has contributed; empty frames before it has.

S5

Set once, change any time

Quiet rows, one sheet each — the look, the switches, the connections. Never above the stage, never more prominent than the parts.

S6

The money card — a filling meter, bought in place

Credits or plan, drawn as a meter you watch move, with the ladder beside it. Only for services that have one.

S7

Offers last · the boundary note

Upsells are quiet tiles at the bottom — an offer never outranks the day. The dashed boundary note names what deliberately lives elsewhere, so nobody hunts for it.

·

The two lives, and the three states

Not started: the shipped App-Store shop window, kept as is — with its stage showing the promise. Started: this pattern, in three states: before (stage shows the waiting roll + countdown), during (stage fills live, counts up), after (stage is the headline; controls demoted below it). Same page, same order — only the stage's contents and the next step change. Small-service rule: S1–S3 only is a complete page.

Worked example 1 of 3 · Papic · state 1 — the state every live event is in today

Before the day — an empty roll is a promise, not a blank

The library is genuinely empty, and the page doesn't pretend otherwise. It opens on the stage anyway: a film roll of waiting frames, a countdown to the day the cameras open, and one lit frame — add the first memory — because uploads are already open. This is the rival category's strongest move (Lapse's darkroom, Dispo's develop clock), done with our own approved Gallery panel.

Papic · state 2 of 3

On the day — the roll fills, live, while you watch

Same page, same order. The stage now does what a photo product does at a party: new tiles settle in as they arrive, one frame shows a photo still being screened — drawn honestly as an obscured frame, the darkroom idea — and the caption bar counts up. The next step knows the moment: "4 QRs are still in your pocket."

Papic · state 3 of 3 · the page they come back to for years

After — the library is the headline, with its makers credited

The stage is now simply the 08-25 "after" drawing in its promoted position: the full mosaic with chapter chips and per-tile credits, the Recap in the next-step slot, the sources as the roll call of who filled it. This state needed the least change — it was already the one state the approved drawing gave imagery to.

Worked example 2 of 3 · proving the pattern generalises · brief on purpose

Live Studio — the stage is the broadcast monitor

The market's control rooms (StreamYard, Riverside, Restream) centre the output the audience sees, keep waiting cameras in a strip below it, and make pre-live the same screen as live with a colour flip. Setnayan already owns that idiom — the approved day-of controller drawing is exactly it. The control centre borrows the controller's monitor as its stage: before the day the stage is dark and says so, the cameras are the strip, and the one terracotta action opens the controller. The buy state keeps the shipped App-Store window.

Worked example 3 of 3 · brief on purpose

The event page — the stage is the page itself, live

Luma's insight, ported: the manage view is anchored by the thing the guests see. The couple's controls for their public page are ~15 sub-pages today, reached from a list. The pattern gathers them under a stage that is a living miniature of the page itself, in whichever of its four stages it is in right now — save the date, invitation, the day, the album. Vocabulary note: "Event Hub" is the guests' public address (owner-locked 2026-08-16); this is the couple's control centre FOR it, inside the dashboard.

Receipts

Colour receipts — every text pairing, measured, both grounds

This page commits to one deliberate look (like the 08-25 file): a white page carrying an obsidian stage. So "light and dark" here means both grounds a reader actually meets — every pairing on the white page and every pairing on the obsidian panel, measured. Gold has no headroom on light tints; on obsidian it is safe — that is the one place gold text appears. Light-ground colours are never reused on the dark panel: the panel has its own measured pair.
