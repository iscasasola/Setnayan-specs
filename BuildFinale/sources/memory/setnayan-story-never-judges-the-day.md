Owner ruling 2026-09-09. **A table with one or two photographs shows no count at all** on the
story's floor plan — and this is the house style for *any* small count in the story, not a seating
special case.

I put it to him as a **privacy** question: a low count plus what a guest already knows can point at
one person (the same shape as the existing "never name the one who shot a photo" rule). He ruled
yes, withhold it — but for a different reason entirely:

> *"this will subconsciously tell them they did not create enough memories for the story."*

**Why:** the rule is that **the story never passes judgement on the day it is telling.** A small
number, rendered plainly, reads to the host as a verdict on their own wedding. The product's whole
promise is that they keep their story; a page that quietly scores it breaks that.

**How to apply:** when a count in the story could read as "not much happened", withhold the number
rather than showing it — and record the emotional reason beside the code, not the privacy one. The
danger is specific and predictable: a future session finds the floor, assumes it is a k-anonymity
threshold, and "correctly" relaxes it once nobody can be identified — which satisfies the privacy
reading and destroys the actual rule. Recorded in the corpus at `01_The_Story.md` §5 and as rule 11
of `04_Consent_And_Privacy.md`, which flags the same trap.

Same ruling also set the floor plan's memory boundary — keep the room's shape and where the
photographs came from, never who sat where, freeze it at publish because the seating chart is
hard-deleted and re-solved. Related: [[setnayan-story-and-story-maker-designed]].
