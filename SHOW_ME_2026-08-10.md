## 1 · The in-event bottom bar — what actually ships today

**On a phone it already changes with the phase of the wedding.** This is built and working:

| **Planning** (months before) | **On the day** | **Afterwards** |
|---|---|---|
| 🏠 Home | 🏠 Now | 🏠 Overview |
| 👥 Guests | ▣ Check-in | ⭐ Review |
| 🧭 Explore | ▦ Seats | 📰 Editorial |
| ✨ Studio | 🚀 Services | 🖼 Galleries |
| 🎨 Design | 🗓 Schedule | |
| 💰 Budget | | |
| **6 tabs** | **5 tabs** | **4 tabs** |

**On a laptop the side rail shows five, with different names:**

> Overview · Guests · **Marketplace** · **Studio** · Launch

### The real problem is not the number — it's that they disagree

The same wedding is described **two different ways**. "Explore" on a phone is "Marketplace" on a laptop. "Design" and "Budget" exist on the phone and not in the rail. A couple who plans on their laptop and checks on their phone is learning the app twice.

### What I recommend

**Keep the phase switching.** It's the better product and it already works — on the wedding day nobody wants a Budget tab; they want Check-in and Seats. Throwing that away to match a drawing would be a real loss.

**Fix the disagreement instead:** one set of top-level names, used by both, with the phone free to show fewer of them and to swap on the day.

That reverses nothing you've locked. Your "no submenus, solid menu" ruling stays. The five-page structure stays. What changes is that the phone and the laptop finally call the same thing by the same name.

**One question if you want to go further:** six is one more than most phone bars carry comfortably. If you want five in the planning phase too, the natural one to fold in is **Design** — it lives inside Studio anyway. Say the word and I'll do that; otherwise I'll leave six.

## 2 · The four photo switches — built, and switched off

All four are finished. None is visible to anyone today.

### ① The host's shots-remaining meter
A card on your Papic page showing how many shots are left in the event's pool. **Read-only** — no top-up button, no purchase doorway. You just see the number.

*Lowest risk of the four. Nothing anyone can get wrong.*

### ② A guest buying more shots without an account
When a guest runs out, an "add more shots" entry appears on their camera screen, leading to an order page that needs no sign-up. They pay, they keep shooting.

*This one takes money from someone who has no account, so their name and payment screenshot are held — which is exactly the person the privacy sheet was just corrected to name.*

### ③ The shared pool gallery
Any guest browses the whole event's photos and taps to link themselves to the ones they're in — instead of waiting to be tagged.

*Triple-locked on purpose: this switch, **and** a privacy control in your admin console, **and** a per-wedding toggle the couple sets, which is off by default. All three must be on before a single guest sees anything.*

### ④ Photo missions and challenges
Auto-generated missions ("get a shot of the cake"), a leaderboard, and challenges a supplier can set.

*This switch also controls the "Photo Challenge" wording on the guest landing page — deliberately, so the feature can never be advertised before it exists.*

### My lean

Turn on **① the meter** and **③ the shared gallery**. The meter is harmless and useful, and the gallery is the one guests actually ask for — and it still can't do anything until a couple opts in.

Hold **② buying without an account** until the payment paperwork is settled, and **④ missions** until you've seen it on a phone.
