The Auto-reply question, answered

It stays here in My Shop, and the Inbox gets one line that points back.The switch, the daily cap and the auto-accept threshold are rules about your whole shop — set once, like your bank details — while the Inbox is where you answer one couple at a time. A rule you cannot see from the Inbox is a rule you forget is on, so the Inbox shows one line at its top — "Assistant add-on · until 8 Oct · change" — and that link lands on this card. The line reads the paid add-on, never the switch: today's engine refuses to answer without an active add-on before it even looks at the switch, so a line that said "on" from the switch alone could be a lie. Door 4 draws that Inbox line so it gets built, not just promised.

The two "completes", kept apart on purpose

  • "Fields in" is a measure. A grey bar, "N of 8 fields in", never green, never a percentage, never the word "complete". It counts what you typed.
  • "Verified" is a badge. A green shield with a date, in exactly three places: beside the shop name, on the "what Setnayan checked" fact in door 1, and on the papers card. It comes only from your papers being read by Setnayan — and the page says so in one sentence under the two facts.
  • The marketplace line joins them. "Showing on the marketplace since 14 Jul" or "Not on the marketplace yet — couples find you once both are done", which is the owner's own rule: papers checked and profile complete.
  • One red button per page. The hero's "Next:" action is the only red button; every in-door primary is ink. Its precedence: unverified → finish details, then papers (first paper · settle a mismatch · the missing paper · send for final check); under review → the next thing that is not the papers (a first card, a way to be paid), else nothing; verified → only keep-up items speak (a permit due within 60 days, a bank proof not on file), else nothing. A shop with nothing to do is told so, with no button.

For the owner to rule — drawn one way, not decided

  1. What happens when a Mayor's Permit actually lapses. The drawing keeps the badge on with a 60-day amber clock and a reminder. Whether an expired permit ever takes a shop off the marketplace is a policy about who appears with expired papers — yours, not ours.
  2. The Inbox line. Drawn here as the answer to "should Auto-reply be on messages/inbox?" — if you would rather the whole card live in the Inbox, it moves in one line either way.
  3. Every number in this drawing is a placeholder — views, earnings, "3 gigs near you", "we answer within three days", the 60-day clock, the add-on's end date. The real ones come from the shop's own records, never from this file. Corrected 2026-09-10: the first draft said the permit's expiry "is read from the paper and not kept". Half wrong — when Setnayan approves a shop it does write a renewal-due date, one year out, and that date is what a renewal clock can run on today. What is not kept is the date printed on the permit itself (31 December of its year); the drawing's "valid until" line needs that stored, or it reads the one-year date instead. Either way the clock has a source.
