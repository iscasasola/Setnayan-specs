Love Story, two voices

"or love story. has both a his and her side. it is like he said she said style. how can we present that kind. so people can both read our love story on both perspective." · "no i mean how do we view this love story scene?"

Each moment keeps its date, title, place and photo. Under them, two voices under the partners' own first names from the event (never "he" or "she"): only the voices that were written show; a moment nobody wrote for shows today's single line. The words are content and stay free. How the whole scene is viewed is a Look a couple picks in the Maker's Style tab (one dropdown); how it enters is Animate. Both are ◆ Pro (2026-10-16); a free event gets the default look and the theme's own entrance, and still gets both voices.

Sample words are invented. Phone first, 375 px. Dark mode follows your system, or the switch below.

Conversation

  • What a guest sees. The story as a thread: a date chip, the title, the photo, then Indalecio's bubble on the left and Claire's on the right, like reading their messages. Oldest first, so the story reads top to bottom.
  • How they move. They scroll, the way they read any thread. Nothing to learn, nothing to tap. The next scene follows below as today.
  • How it enters ◆. The date and photo fade in; the bubbles arrive one after another, a beat apart, like messages landing. Reduced motion: the finished thread.
  • One voice / none. One bubble alone on its side; no voice, the single line sits centred in italics, as the narrator.

Two-lane timeline

  • What a guest sees. One line runs down the centre of the scene. Each moment's date, title and photo sit on the line; Indalecio's words branch left, Claire's branch right.
  • How they move. They scroll down through the years; the line carries on from one moment to the next. The next scene follows below.
  • How it enters ◆. The line draws itself downward; the dates and photos fade onto it; the left words slide in from the left, then the right words from the right. Reduced motion: the finished still.
  • At 375 px a lane is about 150 px wide, so a voice reads at 16 px, around six words a line. A long voice shows six lines, then Read more.

Swipe cards

  • What a guest sees. One moment per card, full width: the photo and date on top, the title, then the two voices stacked, Indalecio then Claire. Dots under the card show which moment they are on.
  • How they move. Swipe sideways to the next moment (the card snaps; the next one peeks at the edge so the swipe is discoverable); tap a dot to jump. The page still scrolls down past the cards to the next scene.
  • How it enters ◆. The photo develops like a print, then the voices rise in turn. Each card plays its own entrance the first time it is swiped to. Reduced motion: the finished card.
  • Without photos (a free event) the card is date, title and voices; the dots and the swipe stay.

Recommendation: Conversation as the scene's default look. It is the owner's own picture ("he said, she said"), it reads at full phone width without squeezing a voice into a lane, and a guest already knows how to read a thread. Two-lane timeline and Swipe cards join the Style dropdown as looks ◆ Pro, beside the shipped Chapters · Essay · Years.

In every look

  • Who is who. Indalecio first, Claire second: the partner names as the event already splits them (the same names the Maker's "Added by" shows).
  • Colour. One colour each, from the event's own palette: the first voice the gold of the "&", the second the navy of the cover's top line. No frame around a voice; a frame is only for something you press (Read more, a dot).
  • Only what was written shows. One voice: that voice alone, on its side. No voice: today's single line, unchanged.
  • Prints and the essay look read one line per voice ("Indalecio: … · Claire: …"), so nothing that reads the story today breaks.

After. Two fields under the partners' names replace "A line or two". A moment written before today keeps its line in a folded row above them, still editable, still what guests read until a voice is written.

  • Which field is kept. The existing line is kept as the fallback ("told together"), not as partner A's. Today's lines are written as "we" ("He asked. She said yes…"); putting them under one name would misattribute them. Nothing is moved, nothing is lost.
  • New moments see exactly two fields. The folded "Told together" row appears only on a moment that already has a line and no voices.
  • Required: at least one of the three (a voice, or the together line). Each voice has the same 600-character limit the line has.
  • "Added by" stays as it is (who added the moment). The builder may hide it once both voices are written; not decided here.
  • Free / Pro unchanged: five moments, no photos, on a free event. Voices do not count as moments. The look and the entrance are Pro; the default look is free.

For the builder

Data: the voicesevents.love_story.moments[i].voices = { a?: string, b?: string }, beside line. Slot a is the event's first partner, b the second, in the order splitCoupleNames(event.display_name) already gives the Maker's "Added by". Slot-keyed, not name-keyed, so renaming a partner never orphans a voice. Max 600 each (MOMENT_LINE_MAX).
Data: the lookThree new values of the scene's existing sceneStyle (today chapters · essay · years): conversation · timeline · cards. Same store, same Style picker; no new column. Recommended default for the Love Story scene: conversation (owner's call; today's default is chapters).
MigrationNone. An additive JSONB key in the one Love Story column; no table, no backfill. Every stored moment has no voices, so every reader draws exactly what it draws today. Seeded (legacy) moments are untouched.
Reading rulereadMoment accepts a moment with a voice and no line (today it drops a moment with no line, place or media). Render: voices present → the conversation, only the ones written; else → line.
Writing ruleloveStoryMomentAction reads voice_a / voice_b; refuses a moment with no line and no voice; never blanks line when a voice is written.
