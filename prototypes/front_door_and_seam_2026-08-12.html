1 · The front door — YouTube's desktop anatomy

Rebuilt against the real thing: hamburger and wordmark left, search centred with its own button and a mic beside it, the account cluster right. A filter chip row that scrolls. Then a uniform four-across grid — no hero, no magazine layout — broken only by a tall-card row and the occasional section heading.

Three things the reference settled that I had drawn differently. ① The grid replaced my hero card. A uniform grid is what makes a page look full — and on launch day it is what lets 33 articles fill a front page that would otherwise read as four apologies. ② A card has no border and no shadow. The thumbnail is the object; the text sits directly on the page. That honours the "never a second surface" lock more strictly than a bordered card did. ③ The personal block moved above Explore — the reference puts the account region second, right under the destinations, and that is the answer to the open question of whether My Home should be pinned higher. It should.

3 · The seam — the round trip

The rule that makes it one product: the rail never leaves. Same width, same side — the sign-in prompt is simply replaced by the account's own destinations, exactly the swap the reference makes. Signing in is the furniture rearranging, not a page you are thrown to.

Going in
1On the public site
Reading a shop page or an article. The rail's second group is the sign-in prompt. Nothing nags.
2They press Sign in
Sign-in opens over the page, not instead of it — what is behind stays visible, so it is obvious nobody is being taken anywhere.
3The prompt becomes the account
The same slot now holds Events · Alaala · People. Everything else in the rail is untouched. Same rail, same width, same side — that is the whole trick, and it is exactly what the reference does with Subscriptions.
4They land where they were going
Back to the page they were on, with the half-written enquiry intact. Only someone who signed in from the rail with nowhere to go lands on their board.
Coming back out
5Inside the app
The app is the app — already drawn and approved, nothing redrawn. The only thing that matters at this seam is the wordmark, top-left.
6They press SETNAYAN
Back to the front door, still signed in, with the account group open and a first row reading "Back to your events." You are a visitor to the public site, not an ex-member.
7Signing out is its own act
It lives under the avatar and nowhere else. Visiting the public site never signs anyone out — the fear that it might is what stops members going back to read the Journal that is carrying the front page.
8On a phone
The index sheet closes and the bottom pill rises. Public site = top bar; app = bottom bar; the two never appear together.

4 · The decisions behind the drawing

Every number was read from the live database or the shipped code on 2026-08-12 — not from a document.

How search bridges the words

Somebody types photographer. There is no folder by that name. The first result is not a shop and not a correction — it is their word with our folder beside it as a place: “photography — in Photo & video — 12 services”. And where no shop exists in that folder yet, it says so plainly and offers to tell them when one opens: a real answer, not an apology.

Three things found while drawing

1 · “Contact us” has no page. Drawn as Help, which exists and has search.

2 · Pakanta has no front door. Sold, on the price list, reachable only from inside the app. Not drawn in the rail — a row that goes nowhere is the one thing this page forbids.

3 · The tools list was short, not long. The concept drew six; eight public doorways ship and a guard enforces the set. Pawebsite, Palogo and Patiktok were missing.

Recommendations — not redraws

a · Match the furniture across the seam. The app's rail and this rail should share width and side, even carrying different content and a different button colour. The seam only reads as one product if nothing jumps.

b · Let the sign-in panel wear the app's terracotta, not the front door's gold. It is the first room inside, not the last step outside — a threshold you cross once, on purpose.

c · The "usable without an account" dots are drawn but unpainted. Which of the eight tools earns one is a per-tool answer nobody has checked.

d · Reading time is real data — already computed, so every minute badge is honest.

e · The publishing schedule is now a front-page dependency. The launch-day grid leans on the Journal completely. It can carry that — but if the schedule stalls, the front page stalls visibly.
