1 · The front door

Sidebar on the left (owner 2026-08-11 — the reference is YouTube and YouTube's rail is on the left). Order and grouping unchanged. Gold actions, system typeface, cream page and cards — this page keeps its own look on purpose.

What the side change costs. Nothing in width — the rail is 248 px either side. What it costs is reading order: on the left the rail is read before the feed, so its top rows became the first thing the page says. That is why My Home is drawn as a block, not a row — it holds its position in the list but stops being one line among twenty. Feed column: 1064 px at ≥1440 (shorts 6-up) · 960 at 1280 (5-up) · rail collapses to a 72 px icon strip below 1280 (4-up) · off-canvas under 1024.

3 · The seam — the round trip

The one thing nobody has drawn. The rule that makes it one product: the rail never leaves. It stays exactly where it is and its contents re-sort. Signing in is not a page you are thrown to — it is the furniture rearranging around you.

Going in
1On the public site
Reading a shop page, or an article. My Home sits in the rail as the sign-in block. Nothing is nagging.
2They press Sign in
Sign-in opens over the page, not instead of it — the page behind stays visible so it is obvious you are not being taken anywhere.
3The rail re-sorts
The block opens into Events · Alaala · People and rises to the top of the rail. The public groups stay, below. Same rail, same width, same side — only the order and one block's contents changed. That is the whole trick.
4They land where they were going
Back to the page they were on, with whatever they were doing intact — the half-written enquiry, the shop they were reading. Only someone who signed in from the rail with no destination lands on the board.
Coming back out
5Inside the app
The app is the app — already drawn and approved. Nothing here is redrawn. The only thing that matters at this seam is the wordmark, top-left.
6They press SETNAYAN
Back to the front door, still signed in. My Home is open at the top of the rail with a first row reading "Back to your events". You are a visitor to the public site, not an ex-member.
7Signing out is its own act
It lives in the account menu and nowhere else. Visiting the public site never signs anyone out. The fear that it might is exactly what stops members from ever going back to look at the Journal.
8On a phone
The index sheet closes and the bottom pill rises. Public site = top bar. Signed-in app = bottom bar. The bar arriving is how a phone says you are inside — and the two never appear together.

4 · The decisions behind the drawing

Every number below was read from the live database or the shipped code on 2026-08-12 — not from a document.

The categories are real now — and they need renaming

The five names in the concept (Photographers · Venues · Catering · Videographers · Hosts & bands) are not folders. But the deeper problem is that the folders' shipped labels are the internal words — the live site says "Look", "Feast", "Documentary", "Booths". Nobody types those. The proposed names below are a copy change only — the web addresses and anchors are unchanged, so it costs nothing to make.

Why those five, when they are not the five biggest. The service count says how finely we chopped a folder up, not how much anyone wants it. Booths has 42 because there are 42 kinds of cart; catering has 7 because catering is one thing and everybody buys it. So the visible five are ranked by what a couple books first and spends most on — the place, the food, the photos, what everyone wears, and who runs the night. Booths sitting just under "Show more" is correct: it is the fun, late, optional spend.

How search bridges the words

Somebody types photographer. There is no folder by that name. The first result is not a shop and not a correction — it is their word, with our folder shown beside it as a place: “photography — in Photo & video — 12 services”. We never ask anyone to learn our vocabulary; we show ours next to theirs. And when no shop exists in that folder yet, the panel says so plainly and offers to tell them when one opens — a real answer, not an apology.

“Trending” over a field of one is a lie, and this page's own rule is that trending is earned, never sold. Below twelve live shops a ranking is noise wearing the clothes of merit, so the heading is honest instead: “The first shops”. Two of the numbers above already match what the homepage ships (it swaps a grid for an invitation below two) — the twelve is the one new number, and it is yours to move.

Three things found while drawing this

1 · “Contact us” has no page. It is drawn in the footer group and returns nothing. Help is the surface that exists, with search and routing. Drawn as Help.

2 · Pakanta has no front door. It is a paid product, on the price list, reachable only from inside the app. The eight tools drawn in the rail are the eight that actually have public pages — Pakanta is not among them, so it is not drawn as a ninth. A row that goes nowhere is the one thing this page forbids.

3 · The tools list was short, not long. The concept drew six; eight public doorways ship. Pawebsite, Palogo and Patiktok were missing from the rail.

Recommendations — not redraws

a · Match the furniture across the seam. The app's rail and this rail should share width and side (248 px, left) even though they carry different content and a different button colour. The seam only reads as one product if nothing jumps.

b · Let the sign-in panel use the app's terracotta, not the front door's gold. It is the first room inside, not the last step outside. That turns the colour change from a mismatch into a threshold you cross once, deliberately.

c · The “usable without an account” dots are drawn but unpainted — the grammar exists, and which of the eight tools earns one is a per-tool answer nobody has checked yet.

d · Reading time is real data, not decoration — the Journal already computes it, so the N-MIN badge on every shorts card is honest and should stay.

e · The Journal is 91 pieces deep with 58 scheduled ahead. The launch-day page leans on it hard, and it can carry that — but it means the publishing schedule is now a front-page dependency, not a side project.
