Three archetypes that carry every logged-in screen.

The shell is the room, the command surface is the doorway, and the states are the weather. Together they govern how all four SETNAYAN surfaces feel — before a single feature screen is redesigned.

01 App Shell

Governs all 297 logged-in routes — 126 couple, 63 vendor, 108 admin. The ~104 public marketing routes keep top navigation and never wear this shell. Never cross the two.

02 Command

Global — reachable from every one of the 401 routes via ⌘K / Ctrl-K, the top-bar pill (desktop), or the search glyph (mobile). Data groups are event-scoped once signed in.

03 States

Apply to every surface on every route. Production is pre-launch-empty: most users meet states 2–5 before they ever see state 1. The six-state matrix below is therefore the most load-bearing design on this page.

Designer's note — the three decisions that matter
  1. The shell mounts once, the content dissolves. Every dashboard route today is a fresh full-page server render — that, not styling, is why the product reads as website. Persisting the top bar, event chip, and nav across navigations is the single biggest app-like win per unit of work; the dashed boundary is the porting contract for Next.js layouts.
  2. Bottom nav is labelled, five items, and only on logged-in surfaces. Icon-plus-text tabs are the strongest convention Filipino users carry from Facebook, Shopee, and GCash — and the launcher grid gives the 12+ services a discoverable home without bloating the tab bar. Marketing keeps top nav; crossing the two is a regression, not an improvement.
  3. Exactly one terracotta action per view. On a deliberately dense canvas (density reads as trustworthy here), hierarchy comes from restraint: gold marks where you are, terracotta marks the one thing to do next, everything else is ghost or text.

Designer's note — the three decisions that matter
  1. Groups mirror the user's mental model, and Actions are first-class. Events / Guests / Vendors / Actions / Pages means the palette is a do-things surface, not just a go-places surface — "send save-the-date reminder" from anywhere is what makes 297 routes feel shallow.
  2. The empty-query state is never blank. Recents plus quick actions load instantly, so the palette teaches itself on first open — critical while production data is thin and a query would return little.
  3. Selection is a gold wash, matches are gold-underlined text. The palette obeys the same accent law as the shell: gold shows where you are and what matched; the only terracotta anywhere would be a destructive-confirm, which the palette never hosts.
Archetype 03 · Every surface, every route

Empty & Loading States

Production is pre-launch-empty, so states 2–5 are what most users actually see. Six states, one surface — the Song Requests desk a booked band opens on the wedding day — so the differences between them are impossible to miss. Empty, Locked, and Denied must never be mistaken for one another.

The skeleton rule

A skeleton is the layout at rest

It copies the real content's geometry one-to-one — avatar circle, title bar, meta bar, action stubs — then dissolves into the data. Never a spinner, never a full-viewport blocker. Click replay to feel the 220 ms crossfade.

Why Empty ≠ Denied is a rule, not a preference
Empty says

"Nothing exists yet — here's the one action that fills this." Centred, gold, warm, teaching. It may only ever render after the app has proved the read was permitted.

Denied says

"Rows may exist — you can't see them, and here's who can fix that." Left-anchored, slate, names the role and the person to ask. Never claims the surface is empty.

The incident behind the rule: a denial and an empty read both return count: 0 with no error — and a production desk printed "no requests yet" over three real pending rows. Any UI that derives state from a count must first prove the reader was permitted; if it can't, it renders Denied, never Empty.

Designer's note — the three decisions that matter
  1. Empty teaches; it never apologises. Pre-launch, empty is the product for most users — so every empty state carries the single action that fills it and the section's one terracotta CTA. "Walang laman" copy with no next step is a dead end.
  2. The three "you can't have this yet" states use three different skeletons. Empty is centred gold-and-teach, Locked ghosts the real feature behind a gold lock with one unlock step, Denied is left-anchored slate that names the role and a person. Distinct geometry means the distinction survives even a user who reads none of the words.
  3. Errors report in three fixed beats — broke, survived, do. Leading with what survived preserves trust on the one day nothing is allowed to feel lost; retry queues instead of blocking. And loading always dissolves through a shape-matched skeleton — a spinner admits the layout is unknown, a skeleton proves it isn't.
