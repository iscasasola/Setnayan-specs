What is actually wrong with it

Five things, all measured. The first one is losing your work today.

1 · Every save quietly erases the notes you wrote

The “what this is for” note only exists on screen while its ⓘ is open. Press Save all changes with a note closed — which is every note, every time — and that row is saved with the note blank. It is not a warning; it already happened.

32 of the last 34 saved rows lost their note · 0 kept one · 32 of 58 rows have no note left

2 · Seven of the seventeen prices we store cannot be changed here

Including the ₱1,499 Setnayan AI sign-up price you are charging real customers right now, and whether a price is one-off, per-day or per-year. Changing any of them needs an engineer and a code release — on the screen that calls itself the single source of truth for prices.

4 rows carry a sign-up price · 7 rows are recurring · none editable

3 · The price history already exists and you can’t see it

Every save records the full before and after of the row. The screen shows you only “edited 2 months ago”. Nobody can answer “what did Papic 10,000 cost in June?” without an engineer reading the database.

34 stored before/after records, none shown

4 · The margin figure is fiction

5 of your 13 live products have a recorded cost of zero, so they read 100% margin — and the tile at the top averages that in. The number is confidently wrong, which is worse than absent.

5 of 13 live rows: cost = ₱0

5 · “Retired” is one tick box, so nothing records why

There is no reason, no date it was retired, and no “this was replaced by that”. One row has the word (superseded) typed into its own product title, because the data had nowhere else to put it.

45 retired rows · 0 carry a reason or a successor

And the reason nobody has just deleted them

This is the important one. Of the 45 retired customer products, 22 are still doing a job: 13 still hold your Papic camera and credit settings, 9 are still listed inside a bundle that is on sale, and 1 is still switched on for a real customer’s event. Deleting those would silently take the settings with them. The other 23 are genuinely inert and have never been sold.

45 retired → 22 still wired · 23 safe to remove · 0 ever sold

What we recommend

The one idea: the sell sheet, and the back room

The screen stops being a filing cabinet of everything you have ever priced. It opens on the 34 things a person can buy today. Everything retired moves to a back room that measures itself towards empty — every retired price either names what is still holding it in place, or offers to be gone for good. Gone is the destination of every retired row, and the screen shows you how far each one has to go.

What changes, and why

Six decisions. Each one answers a measured problem, not a matter of taste.

Each price saves on its own — “Save all changes” goes

This is the fix for the notes being erased, and it is a fix by shape, not by patch: a card that submits only itself, with every field visible while it is open, cannot write away a field nobody saw. It also matches the real job — nobody re-prices 91 things at once. What you give up: re-pricing the whole Papic ladder becomes four saves instead of one. Worth it.

Three states instead of one tick box

Draft (being prepared, nowhere public) · On sale · Retired. Retiring stamps the date and who did it, and asks two optional questions: why, and what replaced it. That kills the word “(superseded)” typed into a product’s own title.

“Safe to remove” is measured, never decided

The screen counts what still points at each retired price. Zero pointers and never sold, and Remove for good appears on its own. Anything ever sold can be retired but never removed — receipts name products in loose text, so the row is the only thing that can still say what the receipt meant.

Every price field comes out of the code

Including the ₱1,499 sign-up price and whether something is charged once, per day or per year. Nothing about a price should need an engineer.

The price history gets drawn

It is already being recorded on every save. The screen just has to show it.

Margin appears only when a cost is real

A blank cost is silence; an entered ₱0 is a claim. Only claims get a margin. The “average margin” tile goes — it was averaging fiction. Max price and min price go too; they answered no question anybody had.
