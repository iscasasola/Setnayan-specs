The same page, held three ways

drawn 2026-08-17 · fable
phone 390 · tablet 834 · desktop 1440
measured, not assumed: sm:124 · md:0 · lg:23

The honest headline first: the tablet is the one width this product has never designed for. The Event Hub adapts 124 times at the small step and 23 times at the desktop step — and zero times at the tablet step. Today a tablet gets the phone column stretched wide. The good news is also honest: the fix costs no new breakpoint. The tablet does not need new machinery — it needs the column to stop stretching, and the two steps that already exist to do their jobs.

№ 01

What is true today — measured, not assumed

Four facts shape everything drawn below. Each was counted in the shipped code this session.

The two real steps

640 & 1024

The page reflows at 640 (124 uses) and switches to desktop at 1024 (23 uses). These carry the whole product.

The tablet step

0 uses

The 768 step is an orphan — unused in the Hub, near-unused app-wide (65 of ~3,000). A tablet today = a stretched phone.

Content widths in one page family

8 different

Eight competing column widths is why nothing feels like one product at any size. The cure is a measure, not a breakpoint.

The bottom bar

pinned everywhere

Confirmed: the bar is fixed at every width with no exception — on a 1440 screen it stripes the full width with five phone tabs clustered in the middle. The ugliest failure here, and it is real.

№ 02

One measure instead of eight

Everything below obeys three sanctioned widths, replacing today's eight: the reading measure (about 65 characters — every sentence a guest reads), the plate measure (photos, venue plates, galleries — a half again wider than reading), and the stage (the widest anything may ever be — the Live hub's wall, the desktop shell). Nothing on any screen is wider than the stage; no prose is wider than reading. This one rule is what makes the three widths feel like one product — and it is why the tablet needs no new breakpoint: a column that knows its measure cannot be stretched.

Owner decision

Adopt the three measures and retire the other five. Recommended yes — it is the cheapest change in this study and does the most. The drawings below all assume it.

№ 02 · Our story

Prose holds the reading measure. Whitespace grows; the type does not.

The bar dissolves into a standing rail. Its five doors — same five, same order, camera keeping the action colour — become the head of the rail; the contents page stands beneath them with a gold dot tracking where you are. The page itself is the same centered column, one measure wide. No third pane, no dashboard grid: the Hub is a place to read, not a control panel, and the host's view stays read-only at every width.

Owner decision — the bar's fate on desktop

Recommended: at the desktop step the five slots become the head of the standing rail, and the pinned bottom bar does not render. The five doors survive exactly — count, order, colour, the locked camera and its spoken reason — so nothing is added and nothing is lost; only where they stand changes. The alternative (keeping the phone bar pinned across a 1440 screen) is drawn in every current session by accident, and it is the single ugliest thing the Hub does today.

№ 04

The same page — an 80th birthday

The universal design carried to a non-wedding, at the same three widths. Same spine — eyebrow, name, rule, gold date, plate, contents, five slots. What differs is the sort: the ampersand block, the monogram, the love-story chapter are wedding-dedicated and are simply absent — not greyed, not empty-looking. The occasion line plays the ampersand's structural role.

The rail head carries four doors, the index carries no story row and no wedding-only entries — the sort and the widths are independent axes, which is the proof the two documents ask for: one design, sixteen kinds of event, three sizes.

№ 05

“In this celebration” — proven at every width

On the phone it is what it was designed as: a printed index under the cover, one tap from anywhere via Home. On the tablet it takes two columns and stays in the page. On the desktop it stops being a place you scroll to and becomes the standing rail — always visible, tracking your place with a small gold dot, the five doors as its head. Same list, resolved by the same rules, three postures. Every rule survives every width: a row is drawn only if the page behind it would let this viewer in; features may be listed locked with a spoken reason; content a host kept private is not drawn at all — no greyed gallery, ever, on any screen size.

Owner decision

Recommended: the desktop rail replaces both the pinned bar and the in-page index above 1024. One directory in one posture per width — two visible at once would compete. The phone and tablet keep the printed index and the bar exactly as drawn.

Above the desktop switch the pills move under the header; nothing is added or removed.

№ 07

The masthead does not grow — the room around it does

The type scale cannot simply enlarge with the screen: names at a quarter of a 1440 screen stop being elegant and start being a poster. The rule drawn above, stated plainly:

  • The names step up once — at the small step, exactly as shipped — and then stop. On desktop they are the same size as on a tablet; what grows is the whitespace around them, which is the most legible luxury cue there is.
  • The gold date numerals already have a ceiling in the shipped design and keep it at every width.
  • The cover plate changes shape once — portrait 4:5 on the phone (a cover), 3:2 from the small step up (a plate) — and on desktop it caps at the plate measure. It is never full-bleed: a photo the width of a 1440 screen is a hero banner, which this design deliberately rejected.
  • Reading text never exceeds the reading measure at any width — which is the whole tablet fix in one sentence.
№ 08

What changes at each width, and why

  • Phone (390): nothing. Every phone screen here reproduces what ships. The phone is the design.
  • Tablet portrait (834): the column is held to its measure with real margins instead of stretching; the cover goes 3:2; paired plates sit two-up; the contents takes two columns; the bar and every touch behaviour stay. Why: a tablet is the magazine held in two hands, not a small desktop — and this form is achieved with the two breakpoints that already exist. No orphan breakpoint is adopted.
  • Tablet landscape (~1112): crosses the existing desktop switch and takes the desktop arrangement. Why: at that width the rail earns its keep, and inventing a third arrangement between two working ones would triple the surfaces to maintain.
  • Desktop (1440): the five bar slots become the head of a standing rail; the contents page stands beneath them with a place-tracking dot; the page remains one centered column at its measure; the Live hub's pills move under its header. Why: a pinned phone bar across 1440 pixels is the failure this study exists to remove — and a rail preserves all five doors, their order, their colour and their locks while giving the desktop the one thing it lacked: everything reachable without scrolling home.

What never changes: five doors, never six · locked features speak their reason, private content is never drawn greyed · the Hub is a place, not a control panel — the host sees a read-only ribbon and no editing at any width · gold lives on hairlines, icons and heading-scale numerals, never in a sentence, and no tint ever sits under it — hover moves a border or a shadow · both themes carry every surface drawn here.
