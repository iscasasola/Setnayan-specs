The shop's calling card, and the page that unfolds from it

A shop's public presence is a well-made calling card. The link preview in Messenger is the card. The top of the page is the same card at full size. Everything under it is the card unfolding, in the same order for every shop — and a card only prints what is true, so a brand-new shop is complete on its first day, never a template with holes.

What the card says, in words a stranger reads before tapping:
  • Picture — the card itself, drawn by Setnayan at a permanent address, redrawn when the shop changes. It carries only what stays legible at chat-bubble size: what they do and where, the name, and two fact lines. Services, coverage and the tagline go in the description, where the chat renders them as text.
  • Title — the shop’s name, then what it does and where: “Tahanan Catering Co. — Caterer · Bacoor, Cavite”. Never “· Setnayan vendor”: the stranger is asking who this is, not who hosts them.
  • Description — composed from facts only. Day one: the papers-checked line, what they offer, where they serve, and the first question to ask. Full: the rating from couples who booked, the booking count, the shop’s own tagline. Nothing here is typed for the share; it cannot be empty and it cannot lie.
  • The wording rule the page already keeps: the card never says “Verified by Setnayan” — a bare “verified” reads as covering prices, reviews and availability. It says what was checked: “Business papers checked by Setnayan · Sep 2026”.

F · Fold 5 · the owner’s block · “Want to add them to your event?”

The couple’s action, the same on every plan and every category

This fold shows the couple’s ongoing celebrations, each with an Add that puts the shop on that celebration’s supplier list, and a create tile for a couple with none yet. Adding needs no message. The couple chooses which event. Creating one asks what kind first — never assumes a wedding. Every state is drawn, because the block is new and nothing else defines them.

What happens under each tap, in plain words
  • Signed out — nothing to list. The tap goes to a free sign-in, then straight back to this fold with their celebrations shown. The create tile still works: it asks what kind of celebration, then sets it up, then lands back here with the shop ready to add.
  • Add — puts the shop on that celebration’s supplier list, shortlisted. Nothing is sent to the shop until the couple writes to them. Today this only ever happened as a silent side effect of sending a message, onto the couple’s first event with no choice.
  • Already added — the row says so and opens the celebration; the other celebrations still offer Add. Never a second Add on the same row.
  • Create a celebration — the first question is the kind: wedding, debut, birthday, christening, anniversary, reunion, and the rest. Today a couple with no event was sent to the wedding setup even when planning a christening.

For the owner · plain English

What this design decides

Everything below is what a person sees. Nothing here moves a feature between plans; the plan prices and gates are exactly the ones running today. Every claim on a day-one page is a check that actually passed, with its date.

What a shop can be proud of on day one

  • A finished card. Their mark, their name in the page’s serif, what they do, where they are — every one of them true, because a shop is only public after its papers are checked.
  • A receipt nobody can buy. Business papers checked by Setnayan · Sep 2026, with the papers named and dated: DTI or SEC registration, the BIR certificate, this year’s city permit, the bank account in the business’s name — each one checked by a person at Setnayan, because nothing here reads a paper by itself or asks a government register. A line prints only when that check passed on a date; the video-call line prints only when a call was recorded. It sits on the card, so it travels in the share.
  • A real year, or none. No year prints until one is recorded by a person at Setnayan at verification — never typed by the shop. Today nothing records it, so today nothing prints; once it does, a new business shows the year it started, which is a fact, not a zero.
  • The first question, ready to send. A caterer’s page opens with “Ask for their menu”; one tap writes the couple’s first message. An empty shop is a working door, not an empty room.
  • One line in their own voice, on every plan. The tagline the page already reads and already uses as the share description. Free gets a voice without moving a feature.
  • Nothing to apologise for. No stock picture (proposed — your open question), no empty stars, no “still has no review”, no upgrade nag for the public to read. The honest “New to Setnayan” line is kept, quietly, under the receipt.

What changes by plan

  • Free (₱0) — the card in card stock with the gold rule; the page composes itself; one centred column on a laptop. The shop’s only words are its tagline; its voice reaches a couple through the inbox, and the page says so.
  • Solo (₱1,000 a month) — the rule takes the shop’s chosen accent, In their own words appears, and the service they want read first leads. This is the real jump: Solo buys a voice.
  • Pro (₱2,500 per 28 days) — the card gets a back: the photograph they chose. On the share card it takes the right third; on the phone the card sits on it. Their best review is pinned first, and on a laptop the trust rail rides beside the prices.
  • Enterprise (₱10,000 per 28 days) — the photograph is the card: full-width, their name in cream across it, Inquire sitting on the image. No photo chosen yet? A dark typographic top, never a placeholder.
  • Videos are on every plan. The Enterprise-only films rack was retired in July; every plan gets one set of featured videos beside their photos, and the design keeps it that way.
  • What never changes by plan: what a couple is allowed to know. The receipt, the reviews, the venues and the booking counts read the same on every plan.

What bends by category

  • The second fold takes the category’s own noun. A band shows Songs they play by moment of the night; a caterer shows Menus; a photographer’s Portfolio leads with a wide frame; a venue shows The space — seats, standing, cars, suites — and its record is Celebrations held here.
  • The first question bends: what they play · their menu · a full gallery · how many it holds. It is the day-one lead for every kind of shop.
  • The verb on the record bends: played at · served at · shot at · held here. The three facts beside their words bend. The price unit bends: per set, per head, per event day, per event.
  • What never bends: the card, the order of the six folds, the service card’s anatomy (name · from ₱ · unit · what’s included · who it serves · booked N×), the receipt, the reviews rule, fold 5 and the message box.

What this needs built

  • The share picture must never expire. Today it is a link that dies after 24 hours, so a share made today breaks tomorrow. The card image has to be drawn by Setnayan at a permanent address, redrawn whenever the shop changes. This design assumes that is done.
  • The title and description of the share become composed sentences instead of the name twice.
  • Cut 1 — the stock photo on every empty shop comes off; the card stock takes its place on Free and Solo. Waits on your ruling — you ordered the photo onto real shops on 4 Jun, and nothing is removed until you say otherwise.
  • Cut 2 — “0 yrs in business · self-reported” comes off; no year prints until one is recorded. Recording the year is unbuilt: nothing reads the paper and no year is stored today; a person at Setnayan would type it at verification.
  • Cut 3 — the empty review block comes off; the receipt goes on the card, and the one-line rule goes above the message box.
  • Close every door out of the app (ruling 1): no email, no phone, no tap-to-call, no tap-to-email anywhere on the public page. Every way to reach the shop goes into the Setnayan inbox, which the page already promises.
  • Fold 5 (ruling 2): the block that lists the couple’s celebrations with Add, and the create tile that asks what kind first. The “which event?” picker with its create row already ships (ongoing and upcoming only, your 21 Aug ruling) — fold 5 mounts that picker on the shop page and adds the create tile’s “what kind?” question. Nothing is redrawn.
  • The receipt reads the per-check results the verification desk records, and prints only checks with a recorded pass and date. Every line means a person at Setnayan checked it — no paper reader, no registry lookup exists; the video-call line prints only when a call was recorded.
  • The native ask per category, and a one-tap chip that writes the first message.
  • Venue names on the record are new data — today the record carries only the kind of celebration and the month. They come from the booking, never from the shop, and never with a couple’s name.
Owner ruling 1 · applied everywhere on this page “our goal is to let them integrate their event with the vendor they find. not to let them communicate outside the app”

So the public page prints no email, no phone, no Messenger, Viber or WhatsApp link — nothing a stranger can tap to reach the shop outside Setnayan. Today every verified shop’s page prints both as tap-to-email and tap-to-call, one scroll above a promise that identity stays masked. This page makes that promise true: the only door is the message box, and the only way to keep the shop is fold 5. Not decided, and drawn as an open question: the shop’s own external website link and its Facebook or Instagram. They are shop content, not messaging, but they usually carry contact details. They are kept on the page and marked as your call.

Owner ruling 2 · fold 5 “fold 5: Want to add them to your event. [Link/Create an Event] shows their on going events and a create event icon”

Drawn as the fifth fold on every page, in every state (section F). About the numbering: the winning draft had five folds and its fifth was the message box. Your fold 5 takes that slot; the message box becomes fold 6, so the door out of the page is still the last thing on it. On a day-one page, where folds 2 to 4 have nothing true in them yet, fold 5 still sits right after “What they offer” and before the message box.
