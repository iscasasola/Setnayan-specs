What this is. Today the Dress code scene draws each role as a flat shape — round head, tan neck, triangle skirt, sash (PR #6137). The owner: "i want to improve this", showing loose black-ink fashion sketches — "was hoping to have something like this" · "but colored" — and a refined fashion illustration. Two directions, drawn on the same skeleton: A · Loose sketch (gestural strokes of varying weight, overshoots, open contours, a marker bleed at the fill edge) and B · Refined illustration (one clean contour, seams, drape folds, a lightly drawn face). In both, the garment is filled with the role's exact hex — colour 1 is the piece, colour 2 the accent (sash · tie · pañuelo · embroidery · bouquet · pillow), exactly the rule roleFigures() already applies — and the ink is drawn on top: dark on the Classic theme, light on a dark theme (one CSS variable; nothing is redrawn).

Drawn from the OUTFIT, never from the role name (owner: "we already assigned different styles to wear for the event" — DECISION_LOG 2026-09-20, dress_code_config.roles, editor /dashboard/[eventId]/website/dress-code). The set is organised by the seven shipped values of ATTIRE_STYLES — long gown · cocktail dress · Filipiniana · Barong Tagalog · suit · formal · smart casual — feminine and masculine where an outfit has both, children where natural (a flower girl's dress, a ring bearer's barong or suit). A role with no outfit set shows NO figure, only its chips and the shipped line "The couple hasn't said what to wear for this role yet." The three bride gowns (ball · mermaid · column) are three ways to draw "long gown"; column is the default (it keeps its shape at 64 px and does not say "bride" to a bridesmaid). Names map 1:1 to the seat plan's lib/chibi-config.ts outfits — see the audit.

Honest

Quality note — what reached the references, what did not, and whether to commission

What reached the reference level.
  • B · long gown (column, ball, mermaid), Filipiniana terno, formal gown, cocktail — the elongated fashion-plate proportion, one clean contour, seams and folds as thinner ink: these sit next to reference 2 without embarrassment. The terno's butterfly sleeves are unmistakable at 64 px.
  • B · suit, tux, barong — the lapel V, tie/bow, the barong's untucked hem and embroidered placket in colour 2 read at every size. The barong is the strongest Filipino signal in the set.
  • A · long gown, terno, formal gown at 200 px — the broken, overshooting strokes with the marker off-register genuinely look drawn, and are the closest to what the owner pointed at ("like this, but colored").
  • Exact colour, both directions — every fill IS the hex; nothing darkens or tints it. Dark theme is one variable.
What did not.
  • A · the masculine figures — a suit's straight edges do not benefit from gesture the way drape does; roughened, they look shaky rather than loose. Reference 1 has no men in it for a reason.
  • A at 64 px — the sketch collapses into a slightly ragged silhouette; the quality the owner liked (the open line, the varying weight) is not visible at row size. If direction A is chosen, the rows should use the B small variant and A only in the "You are …" panel.
  • Faces — B's brow/lash/nose/lip marks are generic; reference 2's face has a personality these do not. The hands are mitts in both directions (a hand is the hardest thing to draw and the least worth 8 elements).
  • The barong's sheerness — suggested by a faint undershirt line, not rendered; a real illustrator would layer the piña over the camisa with a half-tone, which we cannot do without tinting the exact colour.
  • Smart casual (both) — the midi skirt and open-collar blazer are correct but plain; nothing in them says "smart casual" rather than "a dress" or "a suit without a tie" at 64 px.
  • Children — recognisable as children (5½-head proportion, shorter height in the same row), but the flower girl's basket and the ring bearer's pillow are the only cues; the drawing quality is a step below the adults.
Commission or not. The generated set is good enough to ship as the free default — it is a real step up from the flat shape, it honours the exact colour, and it costs nothing. But the owner's reference is a hand, and a hand is what these are missing: confident line weight decided per stroke, not per class. Recommend commissioning line art for the twelve adult outfits (a Manila freelance illustrator, one round of revisions — get two quotes; do not price it from this page) and keeping this generator as the colour engine and the fallback. The ask, precisely, so the drawings drop straight in:
  • SVG, one file per outfit, on a 120 × 300 viewBox (children on the same box, standing on the same floor line y = 290), so the row height is the same for everybody and children come out shorter.
  • Separate, closed colour regions named by role in the fill: fill="var(--c1)" for the piece, fill="var(--c2)" for the accents (sash · tie · pañuelo · embroidery · bouquet · pillow), var(--skin), var(--hair), var(--shirt), var(--trouser), var(--shoe) — no other colours, no gradients, no opacity on the piece.
  • Ink as strokes on currentColor, in at most three classes (.o contour · .d detail · .f face), stroke-width set by class not per path, so the small variant is a CSS change. Face ink may be a fixed dark.
  • Twelve outfits: long gown (column · ball · mermaid) · cocktail dress · Filipiniana (terno · with pañuelo) · Barong Tagalog · suit · formal F (evening gown) · formal M (tux) · smart casual F · smart casual M; children optional (flower girl dress · ring bearer barong · ring bearer suit).
  • Style: reference 2's discipline (one confident contour, drape folds) with reference 1's gesture allowed in the skirts; faces light; hands resolved; ≤ 80 path elements per figure; must read at 64 px in silhouette.
  • A light neutral skin as the default region, drawn so the three tones swatched above can be swapped in.
If not commissioning: ship direction B as the default (rows at 64–72 px in the small variant, the panel at 200 px), keep the three gowns as long-gown variants with column as default, and revisit direction A only as a Pro "sketch" look for the panel — where it is at its best and where its weakness (row size) never shows.
