SETNAYAN White-glove

Vendor · Custom tier · 27 August 2026

You are selling an hour of your own time, forever, for ₱1,000 a month.

"White-glove" is not a vague word in our papers — it was written down precisely, and it is the entire reason Custom costs more than Enterprise. It is four promises: a named person who is theirs, a sit-down over their numbers every three months, their problems handled first, and a personal setup session. Three of the four do not exist anywhere in the product. The fourth partly does, and nobody has ever been told.

Nobody has ever bought Custom. Two shops exist and both are on the entry plan. So this is not fixing a promise you broke — it is choosing which promise to make. You can pick any of these freely and nobody is let down.

The three shapes below are the same choice seen from two sides: what a supplier reads, and what it takes out of your week. The second column is the one that decides this.

What was written down, and what is actually there

The four promises, in the words they were signed in. The state under each one was checked in the product today, not read from a document.

Nothing there

A named person who is theirs

Nobody can be assigned to a shop. There is no place to put a name and no screen that would show it. It is two sentences of marketing and nothing behind either one.

Nothing there

A review of their numbers every three months

Nothing schedules one, reminds you of one, or records that one happened. The only three-monthly thing in the product is the tax filing, which is unrelated.

Half there

Their problems handled first

This one surprised me. Your complaints screen already puts the higher-paying shops at the top of the list, and Custom already sits above everyone. What does not exist: any promise of how fast, any separate queue — and the supplier is never told it happened.

Nothing there

A personal setup session

Originally written as one hour with you when they join. There is no booking, no session and no record of one anywhere.

Your own homepage argues with itself about this, on one page

In the Enterprise list, "Quarterly business review" and "Priority dispute + account management" both carry a coming soon mark. Twelve lines further down, the Custom description says a dedicated account manager and quarterly business review "come with it" — flatly, no mark, no qualifier. Same page, same two things, one says later and one says included.

This matters for the decision because it means doing nothing is not on the menu. Whichever shape you pick, that sentence has to change — it is the only sentence in the product that promises a person will be there.

And the premium quietly shrank by ₱500 today

The signed card built this price as Enterprise plus ₱1,500 for white-glove. Today you set Enterprise at ₱10,000 and Custom at ₱11,000 — so the premium is now ₱1,000. That is not wrong, it is just no longer the number that was agreed, and it changes what the four promises have to be worth.

Shown here, not settled. Two of the three shapes below make ₱1,000 easy to defend; one of them makes even ₱1,500 look cheap for what it takes out of you.

The one line in that table that decides it

Premium earned per hour of your time. Everything else Setnayan sells is software: you build it once and the hundredth customer costs you nothing. White-glove is the opposite — it is the only thing in the whole product where every peso of revenue costs a fixed slice of one person's finite week, forever. That is why the figure barely moves between 5 customers and 50: the cost grows exactly as fast as the money does.

Under A that trade is roughly ₱2,000 an hour of your time, which is a fair rate for work you were half doing anyway. Under C it is ₱855 — and it buys 200 meetings a year you cannot move. Under B it is not a trade at all, because no hours are sold.

Estimates, not measurements

How these hours were worked out

Nobody has ever been a Custom customer, so there is nothing to measure — every hour figure above is a judgement, and every one of them comes from the four numbers below. If you disagree with a number, change it and the whole table moves. What is measured, and stated as such, is the state of the product: what exists, what does not, and what the words currently say.

Being a named person 30 min / customer / month

Assumes two or three real exchanges a month with a business paying you ₱11,000 — a question about a booking, a complaint, a favour. Could easily be 15 minutes for a quiet shop or two hours in a bad month. The number that matters is not the size but the shape: it arrives unannounced and only you can answer it.

A review of their numbers 2 h each, 4× a year

45 minutes pulling their figures and forming a view worth hearing, 1 hour in the meeting, 15 minutes writing down what you promised. A review you have not prepared for is worse than no review, so the prep is not optional. If you think you could do it in one hour, halve every C figure — it is still 100 meetings a year at 50 customers.

Their problems first ≈ 0 extra hours

The sorting exists already and costs nothing to keep. There have been no complaints at all in the product so far, so there is genuinely no volume to price. The four-hour version costs no hours either — it costs the freedom to be unreachable, which is not measurable in hours and is the real price.

Setup session 1½ h, once

The signed papers say one hour. Half an hour of reading their shop first is what makes the hour useful. This is the cheapest of the four by a distance and the only one that never comes back — it appears in every shape except B for that reason.

What is measured, and is not a judgement call

Two shops exist and both are on the entry plan. None has ever been on Custom. There have been no complaints and no vendor conversations at all. The three-monthly review, the named person and the setup session do not exist anywhere in the product. The problems-first sorting does exist, does already put Custom at the top, and has never had two things in the queue to sort.     What makes one of these obviously wrong

    Two things, and neither is a matter of taste.

    C is refused by arithmetic, not by opinion

    Fifty Custom customers means 200 booked meetings a year — about four every week, forever — for a company whose entire admin is one person, and whose own standing plan is built on you not having to be present for things to work. It is also the one promise that cannot be handed over, delegated or paused, because its whole value is that it is you. And the moment it starts slipping it does more damage than never offering it: a missed review is a broken promise with a date on it.

    The honest version of C is not "never" — it is "not while the admin is one person". It is a thing to sell when there is a second employee, priced then at what a second employee costs.

    A is cheaper than it looks, because a quarter of it is already built

    This is the finding that changes the shape of the choice, and it was not in the brief for this page. Your complaints screen already sorts by who pays you most, and Custom already sits at the top of that sort. So "their problems handled first" is not a build — it is a build you finished some time ago and never told anybody about. What is left is telling the supplier it is true and deciding what "first" means.

    That leaves A as: one new list of who is assigned to whom, one sentence in the supplier's dashboard, and a habit. Against ₱1,000 a month per customer it is the best-value promise of the three.

    the sorting is real, favours Custom, and has never had two things to sort

    And B is genuinely viable — do not read it as giving up

    Custom already justifies its price without any of this: it is the only plan that removes every cap, and the only one that can list nationwide or run on the shop's own web address. Neither is possible at any price below it. A price defended entirely by switches is a price that never needs your Tuesday. B is the right answer if you would rather spend the next year building than meeting.

    The real cost of B is not the supplier's — it is that you lose the reason the ₱1,000 was invented in the first place, and the tier has to stand on caps alone from then on.

    Whatever you pick, one thing has to happen this week

    The sentence promising a dedicated account manager and a quarterly review is on the public site right now, unqualified, and it is not true. Nobody has been misled yet because nobody has bought Custom — but that sentence is live in front of every shop looking at your prices today. Under A it becomes a smaller true sentence, under B it goes, under C it stays and waits. There is no version where it survives untouched.

    And the ₱500 is still open

    The signed construction was Enterprise plus ₱1,500. Today's numbers make it ₱1,000. Under A the smaller premium fits the smaller promise, and ₱1,000 is right. Under C you are giving away eight hours of your year per customer for ₱12,000 of premium — which is why C would need the ₱1,500 back, and arguably more. Under B the question dissolves: there is no white-glove premium at all, only a price for a plan with no ceilings.

    This is deliberately not answered here. It only has an answer once you have picked a shape.
