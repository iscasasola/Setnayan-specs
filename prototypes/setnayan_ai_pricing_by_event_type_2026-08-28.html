Design prototype · 28 August 2026 · nothing built

One price band, many kinds of celebration — and every kind belongs to exactly one band

Setnayan AI does not cost the same for a wedding as it does for a coffee catch-up, and it never has. Today those five prices are written into the app itself, so changing one is an engineering job. This is that decision handed back to you as a screen: a price, and a tick-box for every kind of celebration that pays it. Tick a kind here and it leaves whichever band it was in, so the same celebration can never be quoted two prices.

“we want to be able to set all events accordingly. a price and a checkbox. If a checkbox is checked, it should not show to the other prices.”— the owner, 28 August 2026

What building this screen found

Six things, each read out of the live prices or the shipped app this morning rather than taken from a note. Four of them are decisions only you can make.

A wake is being charged ₱899 and nobody ever chose that

Sixteen kinds of celebration were given a price on purpose. The seventeenth — a wake — was added to the product in August and never given one, so it falls through to the middle band. The number is not wrong on its face; what is wrong is that it is a leftover, not a decision, and nothing on any screen says so.

Two questions come with it and both are yours: whether ₱899 is right for a family arranging a funeral, and whether an assisted planner should offer itself at a wake at all. The second one is not a pricing question and this screen deliberately does not answer it.

The next kind of celebration you add will do exactly the same thing, silently

There is no moment today where a new kind is stopped and asked what it costs — it simply starts being sold at the middle price. That is why the tray at the top of this screen holds a permanent greyed row for kinds that have not been given a home: it turns a silent default into a visible question, and the screen is not “clean” until the tray is empty.

“Not offered” and “free” look identical on a price list, and they are opposites

One kind of celebration — a simple event — has no Setnayan AI price because there is nothing to plan: no suppliers, so the planner has nobody to reach. Written as ₱0 that reads as a free version somebody could switch on. This screen gives it no price field at all and says “not sold here” in words, because a ₱0 in a price column is an invitation to ask why the free one is not being promoted.

Four price rows exist, but you only sell one of them

Three of the four numbers behind these bands are switched off in the catalogue — they exist only to hold a figure that something else reads. That is worth knowing before this screen makes them look like four products: Setnayan AI is one product with four prices, not four products. The screen labels them as bands and never as things a customer could buy, and it does not offer a way to “activate” three prices that are not meant to be activated.

A whole-number percentage cannot reproduce two of the prices you charge today

Two of the four sign-up prices are not round fractions of their regular price — ₱899 down to ₱499 is 44.5% and ₱199 down to ₱99 is 50.3%. If the discount box only took whole numbers, saving this screen without touching anything would quietly move two live prices, one of them by four pesos. The box therefore takes one decimal place, and the peso figure is shown underneath so you can see exactly what a customer will be asked for.

This is also why the peso figure is shown and not typed. Two editable numbers that are supposed to agree eventually stop agreeing, and the one that gets charged is not always the one you looked at.

The floor lifts, it never lowers — and the screen has to make that visible

The set-up saving is a minimum, not a rate. Read literally as “ten percent for everything” it would have raised three of these four sign-up prices, taking the wedding price from ₱1,499 back up to ₱2,249 under the banner of a discount. So: type a band’s discount below the floor and the screen tells you the floor will lift it rather than silently rewriting what you typed; raise the floor above a band and that band rises to meet it. Nothing you can do to this control makes a customer pay more at set-up than afterwards.

The words “price bands” are already taken on this screen

There is an existing tab called Price bands, and it means something completely different — what suppliers in a category typically charge, worked out from their own published prices. Two things called price bands, one tab apart, is how somebody ends up editing the wrong one. This prototype renames the existing tab Market price bands and calls the new one Setnayan AI prices. Either name can change; what cannot stay is both being called the same thing.

One thing this design cannot express, and it is worth knowing before it is built

“One kind, one price” is exactly right for what is sold today. But the two prices in each band are not the only two prices Setnayan AI has — there is also a renewal price, and it is a single figure for everybody, regardless of what kind of celebration it renews. If renewals should also differ by kind, this screen needs a third number per band; if they should not, then a renewal is deliberately the one price that ignores the whole ladder, and that is worth writing down rather than leaving as an accident.
