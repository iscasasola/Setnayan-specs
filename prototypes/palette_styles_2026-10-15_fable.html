Arch tabsKeep

Ref 2 — tall cards with a rounded foot, hex at top, a sprig, the name in spaced caps — our own drawing in CSS/SVG; nothing copied.

Tall tabs rounded at the foot like an inverted arch, set edge to edge: the hex small at the head, a line-drawn sprig in the middle, the name at the foot in spaced caps — all in the ink that reads on the colour. 4 per row at 375 (three rows for cale-ice's eleven), eleven across at 1280.

Keep — build second. The spaced-caps name reads at 9–10 px because it is caps on a flat ground, and the tall shape is the same shape as the Tags chip and the standing figures, so the page keeps one vertical rhythm. Beside the AI figures: best of the five — at row size the tabs are 14 × 22 standing beside a 64 px standing person; in the "You are…" panel two 86 px tabs and a 112 px figure read as one group. Risk: eleven colours = three rows (≈ 330 px) at 375 — same as today's circles + legend (≈ 300 px), so no regression.

White / ivory on a light page: Edge to edge means a Cream tab is bordered by its neighbours on two sides; the inset ink edge draws the other two. The hex and name on Cream are in the dark ink (13.75 : 1). Taupe takes the fallback ink (#111, 4.80 : 1) and is flagged in the markup (`data-ink="fallback"`).

Style 3 of 5

Watercolour dotsKeep

Ref 3 — hand-painted circles with an uneven edge, the name in plain type under each — our own drawing in CSS/SVG; nothing copied.

Painted dots: a radial pigment pool (lighter centre, the colour darkened with 22 % page ink at the rim — real watercolour dries darker at the edge) with the edge roughened by one SVG displacement filter (feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap, no image). The name under each in the page's own ink.

Keep — build first. The only new style where no word ever sits ON a colour: every name is page ink on the page (5.4 : 1 light, 8.2 : 1 dark) so Taupe, Cream and any future colour pass without a fallback. The hand-painted edge is the closest match to the AI-illustrated figures, which are themselves soft drawings — the two were made to sit together. Beside the AI figures: at row size the dots become 20 px blobs (an uneven border-radius, no filter — a filter on a 20 px element costs paint for nothing) and read as "paint" beside the figure. Cost: one `<filter>` in the page, CSS only.

White / ivory on a light page: The rim is the answer: Cream dries to a #C9C0B6-ish ring (1.6 : 1 against the page — an edge, not text, so 3 : 1 is not required of it) and the roughened 1 px ink stroke sits on top. No white halo, no card.

Paint strokesKeep

Ref 5 — thick glossy paint strokes overlapping diagonally down the page — our own drawing in CSS/SVG; nothing copied.

Eleven glossy strokes, each a 230 × 40 pill tilted −7°, overlapping 12 px down the phone and stepping 9 px right (a cascade); at 1280 the same cascade, wider strokes (300 px) stepping 22 px — it stays a column, as the reference does. Gloss = a top highlight band, bristle lines along the stroke, a 2 px drop shadow. The name on the uncovered left end of each stroke, in the ink that reads on it.

Keep — third, and the one to warn him about. It is the most "painted" of the five and reads well at 1–6 colours. At cale-ice's eleven the phone cascade is ≈ 350 px tall — longer than today's block — and the overlap hides a third of every colour. The name is on the colour, so Taupe takes the fallback ink. Beside the AI figures: at row size the strokes are five tilted 26 × 11 slashes — lively beside a figure, but the only row look that is not upright, so it argues with the standing figure a little. Suggest the dropdown line says "best with up to six colours".
