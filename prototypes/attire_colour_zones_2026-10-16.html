The rule a couple understands without reading it

The dots are in order, and each dot's little word says what it paints. Nothing else to learn.

  1. Dot 1 — the garment. Coat & pants · the tux · the barong · the shirt (smart casual) · the vest & pants (boy) · the pantsuit. The role's main colour, as today.
  2. Dot 2 — the accent. Suit/tux/boy's suit: tie (bow tie + pocket square). Barong & smart casual: pants. Pantsuit: blouse. With no second colour, the accent is the garment's own colour, deeper (a tie), or the drawing's neutral (charcoal barong pants · khaki chinos · an ivory blouse) — exactly what the drawing shows today.
  3. Dot 3 — the shirt. Suit, tux and the boy's suit only. With no third colour the shirt is white. (A couple says “olive suit, marigold tie” far more often than they say a coloured shirt — so the tie comes before the shirt. This keeps the shipped meaning of a role's second colour: “colour 1 is the main piece, the rest accents”, lib/role-figure.ts.)
  4. Women's figures stay one colour. Gown · terno · cocktail · midi · flower girl · women's barong: the sash or belt is the dress's own cloth in every drawing, and a woman's second colour is the bouquet, not the fabric. One exception, because the drawing already has two garments: the pantsuit = suit · blouse. No labels under the women's dots.
  5. A dot beyond the zones paints nothing and carries no label — it is still the role's palette (the guest page shows every chip).

Zones per garment · the files

Built by zones/build-zones.py from the shipped art (controller-2026-10-12/attire-figures/ src · cut · mask → zones/; the shipped files untouched). Every mask is an alpha WebP, 280 px tall, the drawing's own width. The drawing is re-saved with its recolourable zones neutralised to the garment's ivory (the khaki tie, the champagne shirt, the black barong pants) — so a tint is the exact colour, not “olive × khaki”. Where no colour is given, the zone's default tint puts the drawing back as it looks today.
