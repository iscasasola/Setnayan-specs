The vendor has to say yes before anyone pays.

Today a couple presses Lock and the vendor is never asked — the booking simply appears. This is the missing step: the couple requests, the vendor agrees or declines, and only then does anyone talk about money. The vendor answers on their own dashboard, beside the card they already use to confirm a deposit. Seven days to answer, a nudge on day five. Sample names and amounts.

Why the vendor answers here. Their dashboard already carries the card where they confirm a deposit — the last step of this same handshake. The obvious alternative, the couple's page, turns a vendor away until the booking is already confirmed, which is exactly the situation this step exists to resolve. So step 2 sits beside step 5, on a screen the vendor opens anyway.

What the couple never sees. No "it's final" language until the vendor answers. The card says waiting, and their date is not fixed by asking — an event narrows from several possible dates to one, and only then is it final. Asking a vendor is not choosing a day.

Declining is not a dead end. The couple can ask again, and a second ask gets a fresh seven days rather than inheriting the first one's deadline. A vendor's reason is passed on in their own words, because "unavailable" and "we don't shoot church weddings" send the couple to very different next steps.

Not shown, deliberately: what happens after Agree — that is step 3, the vendor sending payment options, which already ships.
