The moment a supplier is told “not this one”.

Every plan says how many customers a shop may chase for a single date, and how many people it may hold on a waiting list for a date already taken. The numbers were agreed and built three weeks ago. They have never once been said out loud to a supplier.

So the only way to learn your number today is to be refused by it. This page draws the other version: the number is on the screen from the first customer, it fills up in front of you, and the wall — when you reach it — offers you the two ways out and one way up.

Owner, 2026-08-28: use the grid, and grandfather existing suppliers. Anyone already above their new ceiling keeps their setting.

The grid

Two numbers per plan. Chasing = customers you have answered and are still pursuing for one date, before anyone signs. Waiting = people you may pick off the waiting list for a date already booked. Only one row changes from what is coded today.

The wall never touches the inbox. Owner lock, 2026-07-24: “your inbox is never locked.” A supplier at their ceiling still receives every message, still reads it, still answers it, and is only stopped from adding a fourth live pursuit on one single date. Every other date is untouched. That is why the refused card keeps its message and its “Not this one”, and only the Accept button is held.

Grandfathering — the number a supplier already chose

The waiting-list number is a setting a supplier picks. Before the ceilings bind, some may already sit above their plan’s number. The owner ruled they keep it.

What the code does today
Their 3 became a 1 the moment they saved anything. Not a message, not a warning — the screen simply shows a different number than the one they chose, and the choice they made about real couples is gone.
What it should do
You keep 3. Your plan includes 1 — we’re not taking away a number you already set. If you change it, 1 is the most you can pick from then on.
  • Nothing they did not touch is changed. An unrelated save to their profile leaves the number alone — that is the whole of grandfathering, and it needs no new record kept anywhere.
  • Changing it is agreeing to the ceiling. Once they pick a number their plan allows, that is where they live.
  • A plan with no waiting list at all still has none. A shop that stops paying keeps nothing it stopped paying for; grandfathering protects a number, never a feature.
  • Customers already being chased are never dropped. The ceiling only ever refuses the next one, so switching it on cannot disturb work in flight.

What is true in production today

  • Two shops. Both Solo, both verified, both holding 1 on the waiting list, both with the waiting list off.
  • Nobody is above a ceiling, so grandfathering protects nobody today. It matters from the first supplier who sets a 3.
  • Switching the ceilings on changes nothing for either shop — Solo’s numbers are already what they use.
