What is actually wrong with it

Six things. The builder itself is sound — five of the six are about what the screen does not say, and the sixth is a price nobody can pay.

1 · The word "Enterprise" appears once, inside a receipt line

A supplier who is on Enterprise at ₱10,000 and lands here is asked for ₱11,000 with no statement of what the extra thousand buys. There is no comparison on this page, on the plans page, or anywhere else in the product. The only place the two tiers meet is the phrase "Base — Enterprise + white-glove" in the price breakdown, which a person reads after they have already decided.

1 occurrence of "Enterprise" in the rendered screen · 0 comparisons anywhere

2 · What the floor already includes is never written down

The floor includes 100 km of reach, 10 team seats, 8 event slots per category, 300 portfolio photos and your main branch. Those figures exist — they are the minimum value of each slider — and they are never said. So every priced row on the screen reads as a cost with nothing to compare it to.

5 included figures · 0 stated · each discoverable only by dragging a slider to its stop

3 · It calls itself a request, and it is not one

The button says Request this plan and the confirmation says the team reviews the composition and sends payment instructions. The code does no such thing: it creates the order and the pending payment there and then, at a price it recomputes on the server, and the bank details are already on screen two inches above the button. The mechanism is self-service. Only the words are a queue — and they tell a supplier to sit and wait for an email that is not coming.

order + payment minted in the same action · BDO/GCash numbers already rendered · copy says "reviews"

4 · A retired currency is still on sale here

"Included tokens / cycle" charges ₱100 per token. Tokens were retired across the whole product on 7 August. The catalogue row was switched off — and switching it off did nothing, because the price reader skips inactive rows and then falls back to a figure written into the code. A supplier can still put 500 dead tokens on their plan and be charged ₱50,000 a cycle for them.

vendor_custom_included_token · is_active = false in prod · fallback ₱100 still charged

5 · The floor is not the floor — it quotes ₱11,099

Every quote is rounded up to the next ‑99 ending. So a Custom plan with nothing added at all, on a ₱11,000 floor, prices at ₱11,099. The screen says "starts at ₱11,000" three lines above a number that is not ₱11,000. The rounding is deliberate and fine for composed totals; applying it to the untouched floor makes the headline price a lie by ₱99.

charmRoundUp(11000) = 11099 · headline and quote disagree on an empty plan

6 · There is an annual price on the card and no way on earth to buy it

The quote card prints a per-year figure and explains it — 13 cycles, pay 10. The order it creates is always the 28-day amount. There is no annual Custom product, no annual toggle on this screen, and no code path anywhere that charges the yearly number. It has been displayed and unbuyable since the day it shipped.

order amount = the 28-day figure, on every path · 0 annual Custom SKUs exist

D · What changes, and what deliberately does not

The builder is not rebuilt. Seven working controls, the sticky quote, the live breakdown, the payment picker, the verified-store gate and the server-side re-pricing all stay exactly as they are.

The floor states what it includes, and the figures are Enterprise's own

Six included values above the controls, and a small delta under each one as it is raised. This is the whole comparison, made permanent — a supplier reading the builder never loses sight of where Enterprise stopped.

The button says Order, and the price is on the button

The code already takes the order. Saying "request" made a self-service purchase look like a queue, and told suppliers to wait for an email nobody sends. Putting the figure on the button is the last honest thing a checkout can do.

The floor is exempt from the ‑99 rounding

₱11,000 is a price the owner set; ₱11,099 is a price arithmetic set. A plan with nothing added quotes the floor exactly. Everything composed on top still rounds as it does today — the drawing shows the rounded figure in small type whenever the two differ, so nothing is hidden.

The tokens control comes off, and the fallback price comes off with it

Retiring the catalogue row was not enough — the price reader skips inactive rows and then uses a figure held in the code. Removing the row without removing the fallback is the shape this project keeps paying for: the switch flips and the charge stays.

What does NOT change: the booking fee

5% on the first ₱100,000, 1% above, ₱50 floor, no cap — sourced clients only, first five free once verified. Identical on Free and identical on Custom. Drawn as the same row in both columns on purpose, because a supplier will look for a discount there. Owner decision 3 is whether there should be one.

What does NOT change: no discount control for the supplier

The pricing engine has a per-shop discount arm — an amount off or a percentage off. It is staff-side and stays staff-side. A supplier always composes at list.

E · Owner decisions — five things this drawing will not decide for you

Each one carries a recommendation. None of them blocks the drawing; all of them change what ships.

1

Custom has no yearly price. Should it?

Every other tier has one. The Custom screen already prints a yearly figure and explains it — and no code path anywhere can charge it, so it has been decoration since it shipped. At 28-day × 10.4 the figure would be ₱114,400; at the multiplier the rest of the product actually uses today (× 10) it is ₱110,000. There is a real reason it might stay unsold: a Custom plan is a composition a shop is expected to adjust, and a year is a long time to be locked to one shape.

Recommendation

Yes, sell it — and delete the printed figure until you do. A supplier paying ₱11,000 a month is exactly who wants the three free cycles, and refusing them the only discount in the ladder makes the top tier the worst value in it. Let a mid-year adjustment be pro-rated against what they already paid, the way an upgrade already is. Whichever way you rule, the number on screen today must go: an unbuyable price is worse than no price.

2

Should the five add-on prices move too? Only the floor moved.

Nationwide is ₱2,499 — on an ₱8,999 floor that was 28% on top; on ₱11,000 it is 23%, and the pair costs ₱13,499 a cycle. Meanwhile ₱99 per hundred photos is now under 1% of the floor, and a shop can take 3,000 photos for ₱2,673 — cheap enough that nobody will ever think about it, which is either the point or a leak. Extra seats at ₱250 are the one to watch: at 10 included, a 30-person outfit adds ₱5,000 a cycle and starts doing arithmetic about whether Setnayan is worth it.

Recommendation

Leave all five alone for now — nobody has ever bought one. Two shops exist and both are on the entry tier; there is no evidence to reprice against, and moving the base and the axes in the same week means you will never learn which one moved anything. The one I would revisit first is seats, and only when a real shop hits eight of them.

3

Does Custom get a break on the booking fee?

Today the fee is identical at every tier — 5% on the first ₱100,000, 1% above, no cap. A Custom shop is by definition the one doing the largest and the most bookings, so it pays the most fees and the most subscription. The counter-argument is strong: the fee pays for the introduction, and an introduction costs the same to make whoever receives it. Discounting it also makes the fee negotiable in a supplier's head, which is the one thing it must never be.

Recommendation

Keep it identical, and say so out loud on the comparison card — which is why that row is drawn in both columns reading the same. A supplier who has to hunt for the fee assumes it is being hidden. If you ever want to reward the top of the ladder, do it with more free sourced bookings (today every verified shop gets five) rather than a lower rate: a bigger free run is a gift you can end, a lower rate is a precedent you cannot.

4

API access is a real Custom-only capability, and it costs nothing

Found while reading the code. A Custom plan can carry an API-access permission; it is stored on the plan, it opens a real gate in the product, only staff can tick it, and it is not in the price at all. So the single most valuable thing Custom can do is invisible on the screen that sells Custom, and free when it is granted. It is the strongest argument the tier has and it is not being made.

Recommendation

Put it on the comparison card as the headline Custom-only row, and price it. It cannot go in the builder until it has a price — a tick box with no figure beside it in a list of priced figures reads as free. Until you set one, the honest interim is a line on the card saying it is available on Custom and arranged with us, which is at least true.

5

Enterprise is ₱8,000 in the live product, not ₱10,000

This drawing was given Enterprise at ₱10,000 / ₱104,000 and drew that. Measured today, the live price list and the code both say ₱8,000 a cycle and ₱80,000 a year — and every yearly price that ships is exactly ten cycles, never 10.4. Custom at ₱11,000 sits above Enterprise either way, so nothing here breaks. But the comparison card's whole job is to show a ₱1,000 step, and against ₱8,000 the step is ₱3,000 — which is a different sentence and possibly a different design.

Recommendation

Say which rate card is real before this ships, because the card's headline depends on it. If Enterprise is moving to ₱10,000 in the same change as Custom's ₱11,000, the drawing is correct as it stands. If Enterprise stays at ₱8,000, the step-up card should stop leaning on "just ₱1,000 more" and lead with the two things Enterprise cannot do at any price — nationwide, and your own web address. One line of copy either way.

And one thing the design cannot honestly represent at all

The comparison card promises that Custom's floor carries everything Enterprise carries. For the five figures drawn — seats, reach, slots, photos, categories — that is true and checkable. For white-glove support, which the shipped copy has promised since day one, there is nothing to check: no support tier, no response-time promise, no separate queue, nothing anywhere in the product that behaves differently for a Custom shop. It is a phrase in a paragraph.

I have kept it off the comparison card rather than draw a row for it, because a row implies a thing you could point at. Either define what it means — a named person, an answer within X hours, a number they can call — or stop selling it. It is the only claim on this screen a supplier could hold us to and we would have nothing to show.
