Find your seat — in the couple's theme, with your key or without

Today (www.setnayan.com/cale-ice/find-seat, measured on a 390 px phone): generic Setnayan chrome, a cream page, one text box, a "Back to the invitation" pill, and a lot of empty space. It does not wear Indalecio & Claire's theme. Anyone with the link can type a full name and learn that person's table.

This prototype keeps the shipped machinery (the published 2D plan, the exact-name lookup, the tablemate list) and changes three things: the screen wears the couple's Vintage theme like every other guest screen · a guest holding their key (QR · NFC · invitation link · signed in) never types anything — their seat is simply there · the open link stays useful at the venue but reveals only a table, never other people.

cale-ice as of today: 81 guests · 16 seated · 3 tables · plan published 10 July · no venue name · entrance marker off (the map falls back to the default door at the bottom). The maps below are drawn a little fuller than that so the pattern reads; the real page draws whatever the couple placed.

  • Theme, not chrome. Capiz-light church ground behind a sepia scrim (the theme's 88 % scrim), Playfair Display for the table, Libre Baskerville for everything else, Mrs Saint Delafield for the one script word, the lace hairline and the postmark the theme already owns. Radius 2 px, as the theme sets.
  • Do stays still. Finding a seat is a function. Nothing animates. It is presented properly, not left plain.
  • One button per screen. "Show at the door" for a key holder · "Find my table" for the open link. Everything else is a quiet link.
  • The same bar as the RSVP and Schedule prototypes: Home · Details · RSVP · Story · Me before the day; Now · Schedule · Camera · Gallery · Me on the day. "Find a seat" is not a slot; it is reached from Details, from Me, and from the venue QR.
  • Privacy fixed in the design, not only in the database. See "Who sees what" at the bottom and the recommendation under it.

Recommendation: keep the open search, make it reveal only a table. Requiring the key would strand the guest who left their phone's invitation in a chat thread and scanned the sign at the venue instead — the exact moment the finder exists for. So: the open link keeps the exact-full-name search (no partials, no suggestions), shows only the table and the room, never any name, and answers "not on the list" and "not seated yet" with one identical message. Everything personal — who you sit with, the door pass, the honest "not seated yet" — needs the key. Add a quiet rate limit on the open lookup (say, 10 tries a minute per device) so nobody can run a list of names through it.
E

Questions for the owner — one recommendation each

  1. Open search on the general link: keep it or require the key? Today it exists and it is the venue-sign fallback. Recommend: keep it, table-only, as in section D.
  2. On the open link, should an unseated guest be told "you're on the list"? It is warmer (B4) but it confirms to any stranger that a person is invited. Recommend: no — one message for both cases (B3). The key holder gets the honest version (A4).
  3. Names at your own table: first name + last initial, or full names? The shipped table-sign view already uses "Ben R." Recommend: first name + last initial; it is enough to recognise your table and it matches what we already show.
  4. Where does "Find a seat" live? It is not one of the five bar slots. Recommend: the Details scene shows "Your seat · Table 3 →" for a key holder and "Find your seat →" for a stranger; the Me panel repeats the seat; the venue QR opens the search directly. No new slot.
  5. Is the door pass (A3) free or part of the paid Custom QR seat pass (₱1,499)? Today /[slug]/seat is gated on that purchase; the free finder has no pass. This prototype shows the pass for every key holder. Recommend: the table, the map and the on-screen pass are free — they ride the free 2D plan. The paid part stays the printed, branded QR cards. This is a money decision, so it is yours, not mine.
  6. "Walk the room in 3D" as a link under the map, not a second button? Recommend: yes. One button per screen; the 3D room is a nice-to-have on a phone at a wedding.
  7. Theme faces. The brief mentioned Fraunces; the shipped Vintage theme sets Playfair Display (headings), Libre Baskerville (text) and Mrs Saint Delafield (script) — Fraunces belongs to Rustic. This prototype follows the shipped Vintage tokens. Recommend: keep the shipped tokens so every guest screen matches; if you want Fraunces on cale-ice, that is a theme change, not a seat-page change.
  8. Venue name. cale-ice has no venue name saved, so the "at San Agustin Church" line never shows. Recommend: nothing to build; the couple types it in the Maker and the line appears.
