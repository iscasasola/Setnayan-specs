The three things that were hardest to settle

1 · “The right-most can be the tools” vs Facebook’s icon row

Both are true and they do not fight. The rail is the complete list — every tool, in words, so nothing is a guess. The composer row carries only the three you use mid-sentence (attach, deal-or-meeting, offer a service), as small icons with a name on hover. Money tools get no icon: an unlabelled glyph is the wrong way to start a ₱241,200 quote.

2 · Where an opened tool appears

It rises from the text box, sits above it, and the messages stay visible above that. Not an overlay over the chat (you are quoting from those messages), not a takeover of the right column (that column is built twice — desktop and phone — so a heavy tool cannot live in it). One panel, one mount, one open at a time, Close and Esc always there, zero space when closed. On a phone the same panel rises from the bottom, taller.

3 · The AI strip

Suggestions sit on one line directly above the text box and only exist when there is something to suggest — no suggestion, no strip, the box takes the room. Tapping one fills the box; it never sends. Note: what ships today is a bot that posts replies by itself (off by default, paid add-on). A “suggest, don’t send” strip is new work, and the honest one for a supplier who wants to keep their own voice.
