Owner askedDrawnThe rule
“yes. that shortlist area is what i meant.”Couple · the bench — the real Your Team pageA supplier’s conversation lives on the supplier’s own row of the bench. Each card in a category row carries its stage pill, its answer, its standing sentence and ONE Open conversation button. Three caterers on one row is the comparison. The sticky Picks column carries only the roll-up (“2 suppliers replied”) and the booked cards it already had.
“yes, it is fine to show it twice.”Hiraya on the bench AND in PicksDerived once, drawn twice, one control. The sentence “Booked · paid ₱50,000 · 2 with Hiraya” is computed from the conversation once and rendered on the bench card and, shortened to “2 with Hiraya”, on the locked row. The locked row’s chip does not open anything different — it jumps to the bench card, which holds the only button. Two places, never two derivations, never two buttons doing the same job differently.
“texts are being cut. how can we execute this properly?”previews written short on every list, both sidesA preview we generate is written to fit; only a sentence a person typed is cut with “…”. “Guest count changed — now planning for 170 guests” → Guests → 170 (was 150). “Deposit received — see you on the day” → You: ₱40,000 deposit received. “Sent the Intimate 50 quote — valid 14 days” → You: Quote ₱112,500 sent · valid to 20 Sep. The fact comes first, so a cut, if it ever comes, takes the least important word.
(the space thief in his screenshot)tags that carry information onlyA tag on every row says nothing. “Catering” sat on five of six rows because the shop sells catering. The service tag now appears only on a row that is NOT the shop’s main service (“Dessert bar”); the date tag only when the day is within 60 days (“3 Oct · in 24 days”). The tag block drops from two lines to one and the preview line stops fighting it.
“all files uploaded on chat should be compressed and minimum”Files · sizes say what happenedPhotos are compressed in the browser before upload; documents are never re-encoded. A photo chip reads “1.1 MB · compressed from 4.6 MB”; a PDF reads “1.2 MB · sent as is”. A contract is evidence and must arrive byte-for-byte; a photo of a cake does not.
“Filter what their current cards are for… just show the current verdict”All · Decisions · Files above the conversation, both sides, both devicesEvery card shows where it stands NOW, not what it said when sent. The last line of each card is “Now · …”. A quote sent in August that was accepted in September reads Booked · accepted 1 Sep; a meeting that moved shows the old time struck through and the new one confirmed. The standing line above them is those “Now” lines joined into one sentence, led by the same stage pill as the header.
(the words on the cards)ladder words onlyOnly the five stage words wear a coloured pill — Inquiry · Quoted · Booked · Completed · Cancelled — and only on a card that moved the stage (a quote, a deal, an adjustment). Meetings, payments and guest-count changes carry a dated sentence, never a pill of their own. “Needs you” is the terracotta outline and the count, not a sixth word.
“Shouldn’t they message a vendor from the build, or marketplace?”Couple · from the plan tab; the list column stays but is no longer a placeA conversation is reached from the supplier’s row in the plan or from a marketplace card, and opens directly — with the event and the shop already known. Replies show on the row and in one line at the top (“2 suppliers replied”). The comparison — three caterers’ answers side by side, each with its own standing — lives in the plan’s category view, which is the thing an inbox could never do.
“Target date will show who are also inquiring for that day?”Target date row in the rail + the accept card“2 other couples asking about 18 Dec · 1 booking held that week.” Counts only, never names — it is the supplier’s own pipeline. The line does not exist on the couple’s side; a comment in the couple’s rail says so.

The hardest calls in v3, then v2’s

0 · A duplicate is safe only when it is a projection

Hiraya appears on the bench row and in the Picks column. That is fine exactly as long as both are drawn from ONE derivation of the conversation — the same function that builds the Decisions standing line — and only the bench row carries the button. The moment the Picks card grows its own “Open chat” with a different target, or its own count computed a second way, the two will disagree within a week and the couple will believe whichever they read last.

00 · Write the preview short — do not widen the box

The desktop list column is 272px and the phone is 375px; a preview gets one line of roughly 36 and 54 characters. Widening the column steals from the conversation; a second line doubles every row. The fix is upstream: every card we generate gets a preview sentence written to fit, fact first. Only a typed message has no short form, so only a typed message is ever cut.

1 · One vocabulary, not two

The tempting design gives every card its own status word — Sent / Accepted / Confirmed / Moved / Pending — and within a week the summary line disagrees with the badge above it. v2 refuses that: the pill on a card is the stage it produced, the sentence under it is a date and a fact. A meeting never “books” anything, so it never wears Booked; it says “moved to Sun 27 Sep · confirmed”.

2 · The couple keeps a list column but loses the inbox

Removing the column entirely would send a couple back to the plan to switch from Hiraya to Kusina. Keeping it as a destination recreates the inbox. So it stays as the left side of the conversation screen, headed “‹ Plan”, and nothing in the couple’s navigation links to it on its own. On the phone the equivalent screen IS the plan’s category list.

3 · Counts about other couples

“2 other couples asking about 18 Dec” is useful to the supplier and dangerous the moment it carries a name or reaches the couple. It is a number on the supplier’s own rows, sourced from their own inquiries, drawn nowhere on the couple’s side. If it ever becomes a link to those couples, it should open the supplier’s own list filtered by date — never a card naming who they are.
