Stories × Editorial — one day, two tellings

Setnayan's editorial and the host's own chapter about the same celebration — a wedding, a debut, a graduation — each pointing at the other, both standing on the same real day underneath: its date, its venue, its team, its gallery. The browse shelves already ship; every screen here shows only the delta.

The reference mockup shared for this work is a dark pitch-deck aesthetic; the product palette is locked terracotta-on-cream, so everything below is drawn in cream.

New  the delta this design adds Ships  already live — drawn only for context, never rebuilt

The shelf is done and is not redrawn. Three voices, three card grammars, one search, one chip row. This design changes two things on it: the two cross-link chips — already built and tested — can finally appear, because days can finally be linked; and a signed-in person with a day coming up gets one labelled lead row (tab 5). Launch day is the home state; the busy shelf is the upgrade.

What changed · why The two code boxes are gone. The list is the permission model: a host links instantly; a guest or booked vendor asks with one tap and the host answers with one tap; a stranger's list is simply empty — there is nothing to request and nothing to type. The host can also unlink later at any time: the day leaves the chapter that moment, and the film and the author's own words stay. One choice feeds everything — the gallery strip, the vendor cards, the date and venue, and the cross-links — so the same fact never lives in two places again.

One day, two tellings — each pointing at the other. The chips below are already built and tested; this design is what finally lets them appear. And when a day has only one telling, absence stays silent.

What changed · why The chips are the entire visible integration on cards — small, sibling links under each card, never a merged card (the three-voices rule stands). The editorial page gets one mirror line near its credits. Absence is silent: an unpaired card carries no empty slot and no “no editorial yet” — a missing thing is never advertised. And every consent unwinds the pair by itself: if the couple takes back their showcase approval the editorial and both chips go together; if they unlink a chapter the day leaves it that instant; if a guest withdraws photo consent their photos leave every surface at once.

The owner's addition: "if they have on going events, we will prioritize showing similar stories as well" — and his correction: "not all stories will be wedding… each event can create a similar editorial." So the shelf is a mixed shelf of every kind of celebration, and while your own is still coming up it opens with days like yours — one labelled row whose heading always states the real basis of what fills it, the shared shelf untouched beneath it, never a heading it can't fill. What changed · why One labelled lead row, nothing else — and its heading states the real basis of what fills it. On a mixed shelf the kind of celebration is the strongest signal: a debut planner leads with debuts, never weddings under a heading that implies likeness. After kind: near Quezon City, the season, the services used. (The ceremony-shape signals only weddings carry are silent for a debut and cost it nothing — a missing signal is neutral, never a penalty.) The subtitle is the person's own facts — never "people like you," never anything inferred from clicks. The cards keep their own grammars — no fourth card — and the editors' shelf below is identical for everyone: the way back is the page itself, plus "See everything."

What changed · why The same signed-in planner, on launch day — and the row is not there. It renders only when at least three stories genuinely match — on their kind, or failing that on place: one tile under a heading about your day is a fake door, two reads as an error, three reads as a shelf. Below three on either basis there is no row, no heading, no apology — the same house rule that already keeps other thin shelves from promising over nothing. So this is honest with today's one story and gets better as stories arrive — never the reverse. And whichever of their celebrations drives it, it is the nearest day still ahead — finished days never trigger it, and a day with no date yet isn't treated as far away.
