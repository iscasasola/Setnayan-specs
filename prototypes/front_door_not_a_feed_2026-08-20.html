1 · What is actually borrowed

Four things on the live page are YouTube's grammar, one for one. None of them is wrong on its own. Together, with nothing above them, they are the whole page — and that is what makes it read as a copy.

The four tells. A row of filter chips across the top · a uniform grid of same-size cards · a “3 min” pill sitting exactly where a video runtime sits · a round two-letter avatar where a channel's face sits. The last two are the giveaway: a written article is wearing a video's clothes, so your eye says press play and then it's text.
But the grid is not the disease. Chips and cards are how every content page works — Netflix, Spotify, a newspaper. It only reads as a knockoff when the feed is the entire page. YouTube can open with a feed because everyone already knows what YouTube is. We cannot.

3b · The Google constraint the hero must not break

Google turned down our app verification — the thing that lets couples connect their YouTube for the livestream and their Drive for the photos — for two reasons. One of them is about this exact block.

Google's words: “The app name ‘Setnayan’ configured for your consent screen does not match the app name on your home page.” At the time, the biggest text on the page was the tagline Keep your memories. Plan your moments. with Set na ’yan above it — so the matcher read our name as “Set na ’yan”, which is not “Setnayan”.
The front door accidentally fixed it. Today's biggest line begins with the word Setnayan, and the site now declares the name in its share card and its structured data. My first draft of this hero would have undone that — same tagline, same brand-origin line above it, same arrangement Google rejected.
So the hero above is amended. The wordmark SETNAYAN is now the brand line, the poetry keeps the headline, and a plain sentence naming the app and what it does sits under it. Set na ’yan keeps its place, smaller. Nothing emotional was lost and the name is now unmistakable.

4 · Why not a splash page

You asked. The answer is no, and it isn't close.

A splash page is a door you must open before you reach the thing. It costs every visitor a click for nothing, it is the first surface people learn to skip, and Google reads a near-empty page as the whole site — so the eleven articles we wrote to be found stop earning their keep. Every big splash-screen era ended for these reasons.
What you actually want from a splash page, a hero gives you for free. The opening block above is the splash — full-width, one sentence, one way in — except the reader can keep scrolling straight into the proof instead of being stopped by it. Same drama, no toll gate.
