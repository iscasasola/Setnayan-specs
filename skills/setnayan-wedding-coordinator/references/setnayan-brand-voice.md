# Setnayan Brand Voice — Working Reference

A short, working reference for keeping generated copy in Setnayan voice. The full brand and messaging guide lives in `10_Brand_Messaging_Guide.docx` in the project root; read it for the comprehensive treatment. This file is the operating cheatsheet for code and copy generation.

## Core principles

**1. Warm, Filipino-comfortable, never corporate.**
Setnayan is "us" in Tagalog. The product is for couples planning a family event, not enterprise users running a workflow. Default to first-person plural ("Let's lock in your timeline") over second-person commands ("Submit your timeline").

**2. Practical, not aspirational.**
Filipino couples planning weddings are juggling families, work, parishes, and budgets. They want the next step, not poetry. "Send your final headcount to the caterer by Friday" beats "Bring your vision to life."

**3. Mixed Filipino-English where it lands naturally.**
"Salamat po, here's your packet for the big day." "Tara, let's review the timeline." A "po" or a "tara" or a "sige" lands warmly when used at moments where a Filipino would actually use it. Never sprinkle them for flavor — that reads as fake.

**4. Family-centered, not couple-centered-alone.**
Western wedding marketing is heavily about the couple's journey. Filipino weddings are family events. Reference parents, sponsors, the entourage. Don't write copy that implies the couple is the only audience.

## Taglines and existing voice anchors

These already exist in the strategy docs and should be used as voice anchors:

- "Plan less. Live more." (couple-facing)
- "One QR. Everything." (guest-facing)
- "List free. Earn when chosen." (vendor-facing — from V2 vendor flyer)
- "The dashboard you've been waiting for." (coordinator-facing)

When generating new copy, ask: would it sit naturally next to these?

## Don't sound like this

These are the "anti-voices" — sample lines that violate the brand voice. If you catch yourself producing one, rewrite.

- ❌ "Optimize your wedding workflow with Setnayan's all-in-one solution."
- ❌ "Your magical day deserves the perfect platform."
- ❌ "Streamline cross-vendor synchronization for maximum efficiency."
- ❌ "Empower your wedding journey."
- ❌ "Hello there! 👋 Welcome to your wedding planning experience!"
- ❌ "Bring your dream wedding to life with our state-of-the-art tools."

The pattern: anything that sounds like a US SaaS marketing site, anything twee, anything emoji-heavy, anything that tries to manufacture excitement.

## Sound like this

- ✅ "Your final headcount is due to the caterer in 5 days. Want a draft message to send?"
- ✅ "Mariel & Joaquin — your timeline is ready. The team has reviewed call times."
- ✅ "Sige po, Kanlaon Studios is confirmed for 13:00. Salamat for sending the contract."
- ✅ "Heads up — the ceremony moved 30 minutes earlier. Your call time shifts to 13:30."
- ✅ "We'll send the updated packet to your photographer, videographer, and HMUA. The florist isn't affected by this change."

The pattern: clear next action, real information, warm but efficient, family-aware.

## Per-channel notes

**In-app push notifications:** Short. Lead with the change or action. "Ceremony moved 30min earlier. Tap for details."

**Vendor emails:** More formal but still warm. Open with "Hi [vendor name]," not "Dear sir/madam." Always include the specific change and the concrete impact on their work.

**Coordinator dashboard copy:** Information-dense, neutral, professional. Coordinators use this under stress on the day; clarity beats warmth.

**Guest-facing:** Warmest of all. Guests are not Setnayan customers; they're using it because they were invited. Make it inviting.

## Length and structure

- Push notifications: ≤80 characters
- Email subject: ≤60 characters
- Email body: 3–5 short paragraphs maximum unless it's a packet
- Vendor packets: as long as needed but every line earns its keep
- Checklists: imperative, ≤8 words per item

## Things to avoid in generated copy

- **Em-dashes used for drama.** Use them for clarity (parenthetical asides), not for rhythm.
- **All-caps for emphasis.** Use bold or just rely on word order.
- **Exclamation marks** unless genuinely celebratory (a confirmed booking, a successful headcount lock).
- **Emoji-heavy copy.** A single emoji is OK for celebration ("✨ Final headcount confirmed"), but don't decorate.
- **"Just a quick reminder."** It's never just a quick reminder; it's a reminder. Drop the hedge.
- **Apologizing in templated copy.** "Sorry for the inconvenience" doesn't belong in coordination tooling.
