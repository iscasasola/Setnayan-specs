The Emcee’s Script Layer — prep & day-of

He does not write a document. He fills in a layer over the couple’s night, and the script assembles itself. Two surfaces, one data model (buildScriptWorkbook()): ① a new Script section on the shipped Customer Card, where he writes before the day · ② his saved line added to the shipped day-of Stage-Script desk. Everything else on both pages already ships and is reproduced, not redesigned.

Design rationale — why it looks like this

  1. The private treatment is a worded banner, not a shade. The shipped desk already locked this philosophy (“a worded badge, not a colour”). The prep surface escalates it because he is composing here: a private block opens with a full-width ink-black bar reading DON’T READ ALOUD — context only, carries a hazard-striped edge (shape, not hue — survives dim light and colour-blindness), and its write field is relabeled YOUR NOTE — NOT FOR THE MIC so even the thing he types there is framed as staging, never copy. The block is never hidden: a host told nothing is worse off than one told to keep quiet.
  2. “They asked” outranks “blank.” The workbook’s unanswered[] — moments where the couple wrote an instruction and he has not written his line — is the page’s “what next.” It gets a gold-railed summons at the top with jump chips, and those cards carry a gold left rail. A merely blank moment is quiet; an unanswered request is loud.
  3. Only his read-aloud line gets the reading serif. Labels, cues and chrome stay in the grotesque + mono system; the words he will actually speak are set in the pahina display serif at reading size — on the prep card, in the compiled text’s visual weight, and on the day-of desk. The eye learns: serif = say this.
  4. Delta, not redesign. Section ① adds one tab to the shipped Customer Card (pipeline strip and pill rail reproduced from customer-card-nav.tsx). Section ② reproduces the shipped Stage-Script desk and adds exactly one block type — his line. Every addition is marked NEW; unmarked UI ships today.
  5. Vendor-private by construction. The lock line mirrors the shipped CRM-notes copy (“Only your team sees this”). There is no share-with-couple affordance anywhere, deliberately — these lines are his craft. And no guest names appear on either surface: the vendor compiler (compileScriptText) deliberately omits the roster the couple’s own export prints.
  6. Tokens are the shipped Atelier set (paper/ink/gold, warm shadows, glass) with the single primary CTA in the 2026-07-31 mandarin supersede — ink label on mandarin per the AA note in the brief. Gold stays jewelry: asks, progress, and his-line accents only.

The dim-room proof. The venue at 7 PM is dark; the app’s theme picker already ships an obsidian register (glass-dark tokens). The private treatment must survive it — and it does the same way it survives light: words and shape, not hue. The badge stays a worded chip; the not-for-the-mic plate stays a plate with an inverted chip; his read-aloud line stays the only serif on screen. If every colour on this card were stripped to grey, the “say / don’t say” boundary would still be legible — that is the acceptance test for this design.
Type scale is night-tuned. His line renders at 16.5px serif minimum on the desk — larger than every other text — because it is the one thing read at arm’s length under stage lighting.
