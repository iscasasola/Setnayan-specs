The Emcee’s Script Layer — his lines travel with him

An emcee does ~40 weddings a year and says nearly the same things with the names swapped. So his lines belong to him and travel between weddings; the event only holds this wedding’s copy. The hero screen is now the second wedding: he opens a new event and it is already written — in his own words, names already swapped — and he only touches the moments that are genuinely special. Same layer model as v1 underneath (buildScriptWorkbook(), line attached to the couple’s moment, retime moves it). Everything not marked new already ships and is reproduced, not redesigned.

Design rationale — what changed since v1, and why

v1 was a form. This is the service. v1 organised the night beautifully and then handed the emcee ~18 blank boxes and a counter saying “6 to go” — every wedding, from scratch. The owner’s verdict: “the emcee should have the easiest and most efficient way to create their script.” Making a 40-weddings-a-year professional retype his own craft was the defect. v2 keeps v1’s layer model and private-block treatment intact and replaces the authoring economics: write once, arrive written forever after.
  1. The split is already the house pattern — this is its third instance. The activity-catalogue migration states it as the whole design: “the CATALOGUE is the emcee’s craft and travels with him; the PICKS belong to one event and die with it.” Owner, 2026-07-27: “his questionaire can be saved as his template. to use for succeeding customers.” Shipped twice: vendor_songs ↔ event_song_picks, vendor_activities ↔ event_activity_picks. v2 adds vendor_lines ↔ vendor_block_scripts: the library travels (keyed to his vendor profile, no event id by schema), the per-event copy stays exactly the shipped vendor_block_scripts table and dies with the event.
  2. How a saved line finds its moment — a three-rung ladder, shown honestly on every card. BLOCK_CUE’s nine coarse types cannot carry “Money Dance” — keyed on type alone, every program block would get the same line. The correct primary key is his own named segment (vendor_activities): when a couple picks one, the shipped bridge (planPicksOntoTimeline → event_activity_picks.scheduled_block_id) makes the join exact — the same activity_id travels to every wedding. Rung 2: the library row remembers the name of the moment it was written on (label_key), so a hand-typed “Money Dance” still matches — filled, but flagged Matched by name — glance it. Rung 3: day-part lines keyed on block_type, for the framing moments that genuinely are generic (cocktails, dinner, send-off) — and they fill only when that type appears once; a repeated type never auto-fills. Nothing matches → nothing fills. The card says so plainly and promises the compounding: write it once, it joins the library. Every pre-filled line carries a provenance chip; a wrong guess is one tap to swap or detach.
  3. Saving to the library is automatic — and the conversion is shown, not hidden. The alternative (an explicit “save to my lines” button) is curation homework a working emcee will skip 40 times a year, and the whole defect was retyping. So: saving a line on a wedding also updates his library — newest wins, one line per moment — the same “cache, not log” overwrite semantics as the shipped vendor_reply_templates precompute. His library is his current craft, improving every Saturday, not an archive to garden. The absolute rule — nothing personal to a couple may ride to the next wedding — is enforced by the converter, not by his diligence: on save, this couple’s names become slots automatically, the receipt shows the conversion (“Carla at Miggo → ⟨the couple⟩”), and Undo keeps a one-off line out of the library. The couple’s notes and asks never enter it at all.
  4. Names are slots — the shipped precompute-and-substitute mechanic, reused. phrasings.ts already ships the idea: the envelope carries no facts, the slot is filled from live rows at render. Here the library template carries no names; the fill happens per event. Two slot classes, because RLS draws a hard line: auto-slots (⟨the couple⟩, ⟨the date⟩) resolve from event fields a booked vendor may read; ask-slots (⟨how they’re announced⟩, sponsor titles) cannot — a booked vendor cannot read the guest roster, by design, and v2 keeps that instead of widening it. An unfilled ask-slot is a loud dashed pill while editing, a triage item on the summary, and a marked gap in the compiled text. A slot is obvious when editing, invisible when reading: the read view and the desk show only natural prose.
  5. Triage replaces the completion counter. “12 of 18 · 6 to go” manufactured guilt over moments he ad-libs anyway. The top of the page now surfaces only what must not be got wrong: the couple’s explicit asks — including asks sitting on a moment his library already filled, because a stock line can contradict a specific request — plus unfilled name slots, plus moments nothing matched. Everything else is one quiet reassurance line: the night is already in your words.
  6. The private treatment is unchanged — and private notes never pre-fill. Ink-black DON’T READ ALOUD bar, hazard-striped edge, “NOT FOR THE MIC” relabel, never hidden; he is holding a live microphone. New corollary of the travel rule: staging notes are per-night by nature (“watch for Grace by the sound booth” is the last wedding’s coordinator), so a private block always arrives blank. The one box that should not be pre-written, isn’t.
  7. Only his read-aloud line gets the reading serif — on the prep card, in the compiled text’s visual weight, on the day-of desk. The eye learns: serif = say this. Kept from v1, including the withheld-serif rule on private notes.
  8. Delta, not redesign. Shipped chrome (Customer Card pipeline + tab rail, the day-of Stage-Script desk) is reproduced; every addition is marked NEW. Vendor-private lock line kept; no share-with-couple affordance anywhere; no guest names on any surface. Tokens are the shipped Atelier set with the 2026-07-31 mandarin CTA supersede.
