## First — the question: "8 groups, so 8 sessions?"

**No. About 30 sessions if you build everything — but only 8–10 before you can launch.**

The eight groups answered a different question — *which items share plumbing* — not *how the
work divides*. Three things make 8 ≠ 8:

1. **The groups only covered the items that clump.** ~35 of ~110. The rest share nothing.
2. **They are wildly different sizes.** One is a redeploy that takes an hour. One is 112 screens
   plus three redesigns.
3. **A session is a unit of attention, not of work.** Three recent sessions produced ~40 merged
   changes; a single "large" item can be a session on its own.

**The more useful number is the second one.** Rev 1 of this plan had no launch line, so it read
as "launch is 30 sessions away." It is not. **The launch chain is 8–10 sessions.** Everything
else is post-launch and should be scheduled as such.

## Running two or three lanes at once — and the hard limits

**The 44-defect history came from ten lanes with auto-merge armed, not from two.** Two or three
human-reviewed lanes are safe. Never auto-merge a fanned-out build.

**Safe to run concurrently:** the paperwork lane (documents, not code) · every outside clock ·
the render lane vs the livestream lane vs the handshake screens (three disjoint trees) · design
work vs any code lane · the permission SQL vs the screen sweep — **provided no screen-sweep lane
carries a database change while a permission tranche is in flight**, because any migration
regenerates the same single permission record.

**Must be serial, and the shared thing that forces it:**
- Anything touching the permission record — one record, regenerated per change.
- The heading conversion and the redesign **of the same screen** — one lane per surface.
- Any two changes that move tests — the coverage ratchet makes them merge-order-dependent.
  **This is the mechanical reason the fan-out shipped 44 defects.**
- Bot protection's three deploys.
- 112 alone. 111 one switch at a time. 103 a few areas at a time.
- The livestream delete strictly after the hand-out and one real broadcast.

## The three standing gates

1. **Never auto-merge a fanned-out build.**
2. **Nothing that charges money switches on without the owner saying so** — the booking fee has
   two switches and has never charged anyone.

   three items as unscheduled — the homepage name, the Google-access page and the dead render
   worker — and **all three had already shipped.** It had read the documents; the documents were
   stale. Every block above starts by opening what exists.
