## JOB A — why it must not be built

### 1 · The premise is refuted by an owner ruling
`event_vendors.status` will **NOT** come to mean "the vendor agreed." The owner's own rule — quoted in `Explore_Replan_BUILD_SPEC_2026-07-27.md` §7 and stated in the deposit migration's header — is that handshake states live in **NEW columns, never by repurposing `status`**. So a guard on `status` protects a column whose meaning is not changing. PR-H's own review finding on this was built on the same false premise.

### 2 · It would break the product on day one
**SEVEN legitimate couple-facing writers** put a confirmed status on the row while running as the `authenticated` role: `finalizeVendor` · `updateVendorStatus` · the chat lock · `lockPackage` · and three wizard locks. A guard keyed on `current_user` refusing `authenticated → confirmed` refuses all of them.

### 3 · Four more ways it fails, each verified
- **The package lock is a multi-row INSERT** (1 anchor + N covered, all pre-set to `contracted`). A per-row RAISE fails the whole package and triggers the code's own rollback, **deleting the booking row**.
- **`acquire_service_time_slot` is a couple-callable SECURITY DEFINER lane to `contracted`.** Inside a DEFINER function `current_user` becomes the owner, so the guard **never fires** there. It is also `EXECUTE`-granted to `anon`.
- **With the payment-gated lock flag on, real money moves BEFORE the blocked write.** The couple pays the vendor and uploads proof; the guard then refuses to record the booking. **A couple who has paid ends up with no booking and no deposit record.**
- **The DB suite passes a naive guard UNCHANGED** — every confirmed-status write in it runs as the migration owner. Green CI would be zero evidence.

### 4 · And it is not urgent
Prod has **zero forged rows**, and an existing live trigger already closes the only forgery shape that matters. Nothing is exposed today.

### ✅ What to do instead
Ship the guard as the **LAST commit of PR-H**, once `lock_agreed_at` exists — so the trigger can require the **vendor's actual stamp** rather than guessing from the caller's role. Two things split out as their own small PRs, independent of PR-H:
1. `acquire_service_time_slot` — re-verify the live body, then **REVOKE its `anon` EXECUTE** (it is on the 190-unreviewed anon-callable list).
2. The completion columns (`service_marked_complete_at`, `completion_status`) are vendor-set **by convention only** — the couple has an unrestricted write path. A couple can forge "the vendor delivered" without touching `status` at all.

## JOB B — build the safe half

### 🔴 THE CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The resolver has **no concept of PARTIAL permission**, and `denied` outranks `count`. So passing `readPermitted: false` on a surface whose SELECT policy has **more than one arm** HIDES rows the reader is legitimately permitted to see — **strictly worse than today's bug**. Today a user is wrongly told a list is empty; that change would wrongly tell them they are not allowed, while hiding real data.

**Rule: only convert a surface whose SELECT policy has exactly ONE arm reachable by that surface's reader.**

### Build rules
- Derive the permission proof **per TABLE**, mirroring that table's own policy arms — **never** a shared `member_type === 'couple'` helper. That uniform helper is wrong for at least 3 of the 8 targets and IS the new lie.
- Generate the couple-only table list from `pg_policies`, do not hand-type it (two hand-typed things drift together).
- Copy the shape of the shipped `coordinator-money-scope.ts` (gate-free core + thin wrapper + fail-closed docblock). A permission producer already ships — this is a copy job, not a design job.
- Derive the proof from `current_couple_event_ids()`, **not** the layout's role verdict — that read's failure is swallowed, so a transient error would show a DeniedState to the event's own owner.
- Use the **caller's own client** for the probe. Do NOT reach for `createAdminClient()` on a render path; state the termination argument in the docblock so the next reader does not.

### ⛔ Explicitly OUT of scope
- **Budget and the schedule prep agenda** — `/budget` is mid-flight in the flag-dark BUD stream that shipped twice today, and the plan would touch the exact read BUD-2 rewired. Hand it over as a note; do not open a competing PR.
- **Receipts** — `receipts.length === 0` has THREE distinct causes and in one the query never runs. Needs a policy fix first (co-hosts are denied their own event's receipts).
- **Vendor 'My Customers'** — misdiagnosed. The list is a MERGE of two reads with different RLS, so a non-owner team seat gets a **partially truncated list that renders as the ideal state** — never empty, never denied. Fix the SOURCE (widen the pool-bookings read to `current_vendor_profile_ids()`, as every sibling vendor table already does) — that is a privacy call for the owner.

### ✅ Safe to build now
- The **four single-arm targets** (per the per-table predicate map).
- **Guests** — an **ErrorState-only** change needing no permission work at all. But fix it in the SHARED helper's return type, not at one call site, or every other consumer keeps the lie.
- ⚠ **Contracts** carries a ROW filter inside its policy that no surface-level proof can see — pass the honest `verifiedNote` override (permission proven, drafts excluded by design) or drop the audit line there.

### 🪤 One hazard the scope got BACKWARDS
It claimed prod cannot reproduce any of this ("zero moderator rows, zero vendor team rows"). **That is false.** The top two targets are reproducible in prod TODAY via the existing vendor-admin team seat and the accepted `edit_all` moderator. **Ask the owner to look at both screens before and after** — that is the verification that actually catches these.
