Iteration 0000 · Guided Home design philosophy

A person getting married has so much to think about.

Setnayan's job is to take that load off — not add to it. The Guided Home design follows from one core principle: tell the couple the ONE thing they should do today, not the 12 things they could do.

The previous bottom-nav-tab approach made couples navigate by mental model — "is this in Guests or Settings or Services?" The Guided Home flips this: the app guides the couple with active recommendations. Browsing all features is still possible (one tap behind "Manage all your wedding details →") but it's not the default surface.

Five design principles, applied throughout

1
One next step The "Next up" card is the most prominent thing on the page. Single CTA. Always action-oriented ("Send your save-the-dates," "Book your photographer," "Confirm your venue"). What appears here is computed from the wedding planning checklist (iteration 0016) based on the couple's date, completion state, and what's most time-sensitive.
2
Progress as reassurance "You're 43% of the way there. You're doing great." Anxiety is the enemy of wedding planning. Every screen shows progress because progress reduces panic. Numbers go up, never down. Achievements get celebrated, not buried.
3
Scannable secondaries "Also worth doing soon" is a list, not a grid. Couple can read it in 5 seconds. Each item: title + one-line context + cost pill ("Free" or "₱2,500"). Designed for at-a-glance triage — they pick what catches their eye, ignore the rest, come back tomorrow.
4
At-a-glance, not detail-deep Three big numbers (Guests / Vendors / Days to next event) tell the whole story. No tables, no filters, no drill-downs on the home page. Tap any glance card to drill into that section's detail view.
5
Browse is hidden, action is featured "Manage all your wedding details →" is a single text link, not a 12-card grid. Couples who want to browse Guests / Vendors / Schedule / Services / Mood Board / etc. tap that link. But the default surface is action-driven, not feature-driven.

What "Next up" actually shows at different stages

The Wedding Planning Progress engine (iteration 0016) computes the "Next up" prompt based on the couple's wedding date, what they've completed, and what's most time-sensitive. Sample prompts at each stage:

12 months out: "Lock in your wedding date." → Set wedding date in Settings.
9 months out: "Add your guest list." → Open Guests.
6 months out: "Send your save-the-dates." → Open invitation editor. (← this preview's example)
3 months out: "Build your seating chart." → Open Seating.
1 month out: "Get your marriage license." → Open Settings checklist.
1 week out: "Confirm with all vendors." → Open Vendors checklist.
Day of: "Open the broadcaster console." → Open Panood.
Day after: "Send thank-you notes." → Open invitation editor.

No bottom navigation

The Guided Home replaces the bottom-nav approach entirely. Couples don't tap tabs to navigate — they tap the action that's recommended to them, or they tap "Manage all your wedding details →" to browse. This pattern matches Apple's iOS Focus mode, Linear's Inbox, and Notion's Home — all of which prioritize "what needs your attention" over "here's everything you could do."

The trade-off: once a couple is INSIDE a section (e.g., Guests), they need a clear way back. A back button in the top-left of every section page handles this. The chrome (event pill + wallet + avatar) stays consistent across all sections so couples never feel lost.

What this implies for iteration scope

0000 ships the Guided Home shell, the chrome, the section-page back button pattern, and the "Manage all your wedding details" expanded grid (showing all features when couples want to browse). 0016 ships the Progress engine that powers the "Next up" prompt + "Also worth doing" list + at-a-glance stats. Both are V1 launch-blocking — the home page is the first thing couples see and it has to feel right.
