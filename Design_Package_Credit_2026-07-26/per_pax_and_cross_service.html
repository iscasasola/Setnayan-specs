Per-head choices, and one package across your services

Two things you asked about. The first has a clean mathematical answer; the second is what turns a multi-service vendor into a genuine one-stop package.

1 · Yes — and it should happen by itself

If guest 101 eats the premium seafood, they cost the caterer more than a guest eating the base menu. So the extra-guest rate must move with the menu choice. The trick is to price the choice per head rather than as a lump — then the extras follow automatically, with nothing extra for the vendor to maintain.

Why it works

Price the upgrade per head, not as a lump“Premium seafood +₱800 per guest” instead of “+₱80,000”. At 100 guests it’s the same ₱80,000 — but now guest 101 automatically costs ₱1,200 + ₱800.
The vendor types one numberThey never maintain a separate extras table per menu. One per-head delta drives both the upgrade and the extra-guest rate.
It also fixes the headcount problemChange the guest count and everything re-prices correctly, because nothing was ever stored as a frozen total.

2 · One package across everything you sell

A caterer who also runs a mobile bar and lights & sound shouldn’t sell three packages. They build one, pulling lines from any of their services — and the couple’s credit spends across all of them. Toggle a service to see the package and the credit change.

What this unlocks

Credit that actually has somewhere to goA credit pool is only meaningful if there’s something to spend it on. A vendor with three services gives every couple a real configuration space — by doing nothing extra.
The lines are pointers, not copiesPulled from the live service card, so a price change updates everywhere instead of drifting into stale duplicates.
One booking, not threeThe couple books once. The vendor sees one job with everything in it — which is the whole reason they’d bundle in the first place.
Both answers are the same answer. Nothing is stored as a finished number — a line knows how it is priced and works itself out against the couple’s real guest count, hours and choices. That’s why the extra-guest rate can follow the menu, and why one package can span three services without a second pricing system.
