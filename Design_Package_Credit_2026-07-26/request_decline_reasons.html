Why it's built this way

Tap, don’t typeFour canned reasons cover almost everything a PH vendor actually says no to. The free-text line is there, but optional — so a busy caterer answers three requests in ten seconds instead of writing three messages.
A bare “can’t do” costs more than it savesWithout a reason the couple asks again in chat, and the structure leaks back into a conversation nobody can act on. One tap prevents that whole round-trip.
“Here’s what we can do” is the money answerMost nos in catering aren’t really nos — they’re “not that, but this”. Turning a decline into a counter-offer keeps the deal alive and gives the couple something to accept.
Nothing is left hangingUnanswered requests stay visibly pending on both sides, and the vendor can’t send a proposal while one is open.
The rule underneath. Every request ends in a state both sides can see — accepted, priced, offered instead, or declined with a reason. No request simply evaporates, which is exactly what happens today when this conversation lives in chat.
