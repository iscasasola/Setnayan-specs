One primitive, three very different vendors

A caterer’s 5-course dinner, a coffee booth’s “pick 5”, and a band’s “add a musician” look like three features. They’re one — a choice group with three dials. Everything below is live.

The whole model

Group { pick_min, pick_max, options[] } Option { label, price_delta, max_qty?, nested_groups? }

pick 1–1 → radio (main course).  pick 5–5 → “choose 5 coffees”.  max_qty → a stepper priced per unit (musicians, hours).  nested_groups → picking the 5-course dinner opens its five courses. Same table, same maths, three products.

Customize package
Platinum Wedding · Sofitel · ₱1,400,000

What’s happening

Pick Plated 5-course and five sub-groups appear — one per course. The couple chooses each dish; most cost nothing, a few carry a delta.

Nesting is capped at one levelA group can open sub-groups. Those can’t open more. Beyond that the vendor is building a menu system, not a package — and the couple is filling in a form.
Free choices are still choicesMost courses cost ₱0 — they exist to capture the couple’s pick so the caterer knows what to cook. Preference capture and pricing are the same mechanism.
The button never liesSub-picks feed the same total. If a course upgrade tips you past your credit, the number on the button moves the instant it happens.

Coffee Cart · Kape Manila · ₱45,000

What’s happening

The booth serves five drinks on the night. The couple picks which five from twelve — and can go past five if they’ll pay for the extra.

pick_min = pick_max = 5The counter reads “3 of 5 chosen” and turns green at five. Below five the couple can’t lock — it’s an unfinished order, not a cheaper one.
Going over is allowed, and pricedA sixth drink costs ₱2,500. Same overspend path as everything else — it lands on the total, in gold.
Why not just checkboxesCheckboxes say “take what you like”. This is “choose exactly five”, which is a different promise — and the vendor priced it that way.

Reception Set · The Manila Sessions · ₱85,000

What’s happening

A band doesn’t swap inclusions — it scales them. More musicians, more hours, more songs. Each is an option with a per-unit price and a ceiling the vendor sets.

max_qty is the vendor’s real limit“Up to 4 extra musicians” isn’t a UI nicety — it’s how many people they can actually bring. The stepper stops there.
This is the upsell, and it should feel like oneNothing here is a swap; every tap adds money. So the meter shows overspend immediately rather than pretending credit is involved.
Same primitiveA stepper is just an option whose pick count can exceed one. No new table, no new maths.
Why this matters beyond neatness. Three tables would mean three vendor builders, three couple UIs and three sets of pricing bugs. One primitive means a caterer, a coffee cart and a band all learn the same screen — and every new vendor type we meet is a configuration, not a release.
