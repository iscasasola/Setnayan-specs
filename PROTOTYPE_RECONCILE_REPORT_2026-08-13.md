## What was deliberately LEFT

1. **168 decorative colour literals.** The Kasama wine/floral accents, category tints, the control
   room's deep blacks. **Recolouring illustration is the redraw this job forbids.**
2. **Two files keep a dark theme**, because each draws a **visible light/dark switch**
   (`floor_plan_tables_vendors`, `guests_living_roster`). Removing a drawn control is a
   composition change. Both themes are on the correct scale.
3. **Two files stay dark throughout** — the Live Studio control room and branding models are dark
   *surfaces*, not a mode.
4. **Focus rings stay gold.** The lock permits gold for UI, and changing a focus treatment is a
   behaviour decision, not a repaint.

## ⚠ The shell requirement — MEASURED, NOT ACTED ON. This one needs your word.

The brief also says *"reconcile to the shipped shell, not just the colours."* **I did not
restructure anything, and I want to be explicit that this part is not done.**

**Verified first:** `SidebarShell` ships at `apps/web/app/_components/nav/sidebar-shell.tsx`,
has **20 consumers**, and is mounted in **five** layouts — admin · dashboard · dashboard/(account)
· dashboard/[eventId] · vendor-dashboard. (The brief named two; it is five.) Its contract is a
**collapsible** sidebar with the state persisted, plus a top bar that **hides on scroll**.

**Why I stopped there.** Adding a collapse affordance to a drawn sidebar **is a composition
change** — it puts a control on screen that the approved drawing does not have. The brief's own
first rule calls that a defect, and the owner has paid twice for a redrawn page. So the three
dashboard-family drawings are **reported, not rewritten.**

🔑 **The real risk this protects against is the opposite one:** a porting session reads
`event_dashboard_v2`, sees a hand-drawn sidebar, and **rebuilds the shell** — which is exactly
`design#3`, the PREMISE-FALSIFIED unit. **For those three files the chrome is not theirs to
build; it comes from the shipped shell.** That sentence is the deliverable here.
