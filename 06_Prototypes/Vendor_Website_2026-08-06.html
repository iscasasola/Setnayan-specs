The vendor's public page — what a couple actually sees

One page, three states of the same shop: brand-new · filling in · fully built. Phone view, because that is how couples open a supplier link.

What this is

The page a stranger lands on when they search a supplier's name or get a link forwarded on Messenger. It already ships. This is not a redraw — it is the same page with five things changed.

What changed vs today

  • A new or half-filled shop now reads as a short, confident calling card instead of a full template with holes in it.
  • Inquire always opens a working message box — even when the vendor has posted no prices yet. Today it can point at nothing.
  • The five stacked chips become the four-stat row you locked — rating · events · saved · viewers (viewers only the vendor sees). A number with nothing to say does not appear at all. No zeros, ever.
  • Every heading is event-neutral — we sell into 16 kinds of event, not just weddings.

Four things cut for good

  • The stock photo on every empty shop — the same picture on every new supplier reads as a scam pattern.
  • "Reviews unlock when this vendor upgrades their Setnayan plan." — it tells couples the supplier hasn't paid us, in the most trusted spot on the page.
  • "0 yrs in business" — an honest new business publicly branded as having none.
  • Raw database words printed as service names — a couple today can literally read pabati on a live page.

Two things I need from you

1 · Nobody can see any of this yet. Every shop is still switched off in production, so no couple has ever opened one of these pages. Flip one real supplier to visible and we can judge the design on a real screen instead of a picture.

2 · Should reviews depend on the vendor's plan? Two of your own rules disagree. My call: reviews never depend on the plan — trust belongs to the marketplace, not the subscription. Only the extras stay paid (the pinned review, the two-column layout). That reverses an older rule, so it needs your word.

And on a laptop — the two-column composition you approved

On a phone everything stacks in one column, for every plan. On a wide screen, paying shops get the trust rail on the right — rating, venues, reviews and partners stay beside the prices the whole way down, instead of being five scrolls below them. This is the shape that was approved and never got built; the narrow strip that ships today only holds a button.

The one moment this page has to win

A couple here has already been burned by fake Facebook reviews and suppliers who stopped replying. Stars can be bought anywhere. A named venue and a date cannot. That is why every review carries the line "Verified wedding · Shangri-La Mactan, March 2026", why the card says "Booked 34× on Setnayan", and why the empty shop still gets a receipt of its own — "Business documents verified by Setnayan, July 2026." One scroll before we ask them to press Inquire, Setnayan shows a fact that could not have been faked.
