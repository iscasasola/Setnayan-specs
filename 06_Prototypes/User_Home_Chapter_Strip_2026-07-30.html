Setnayan · Alaala · the Chapter Strip

FamilyAlbum writes the age on the photograph. We write the chapter.

Their strip moves you sideways through months, with a child's age stamped on the hero picture. Ours moves you sideways through chapters — the events themselves — with the elapsed time stamped in the same place. Newest on the left. Tapping a pill and swiping the card do the same thing. A person's storyline is simply their chapters: the events where they're the honoree.

No face rail. FamilyAlbum hasn't got one either — all their children share one undivided timeline, and per-child views are a paid face-recognition feature. Ours uses name chips, because names are what we have consent to show across people.

Inverted or refused
  • Their white photo-first ground → warm paper + glass; their azure → gold #A9834B, the only action colour.
  • Their axis is the month — their loudest complaint. Ours is the chapter, the only unit our data can fill and the only one carrying the permission rules.
  • Their stamp comes from a child's birthdate. Ours comes from the event date — birthdates are fenced and counsel-gated; event dates are not.
  • No face rail, no avatars. Name chips only.
  • No paywalled per-person view, no emoji reactions, no auto-playing "On this day".

The empty state is the make-or-break. FamilyAlbum's real trick is that it registers a child's name and birthdate before a single photo exists, so the home can always draw a name and an age. We do the same with a different noun — we already capture an event name and date at signup. On day one the countdown is the content. Try "Brand new" above: one card, no strip, no grid, no avatars — never an empty shelf labelled "Memories".

The storyline join is real: events.honoree_dependent_id + its index, migration 20270821100000_life_event_gate_honoree.sql. No new table, no face matching. That column's own header marks it counsel-gated — so the strip, the card and the stamp ship first; the person filter waits.
