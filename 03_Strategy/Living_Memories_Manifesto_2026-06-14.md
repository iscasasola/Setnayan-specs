# Living Memories — Setnayan Brand Manifesto

> Owner-stated 2026-06-14. The umbrella idea over Papic · Live Studio · Kwento · Editorial. This is the brand-level *why* meant to be **shared with the world** — the public manifesto that asks couples to embrace a new concept of memory. Public-surface voice: sell the *feeling*, never the implementation ([[feedback_setnayan_public_surface_hygiene]]); plain, warm, Filipino ([[feedback_setnayan_explain_plain_english]]); EN-primary, Taglish-warm.

## The thesis (one sentence)

The way we keep weddings has evolved — **paper albums → digital albums → living memories.** Photo prints and digital albums both froze the day into still frames chosen by one camera. Technology can finally hold what a wedding actually *is* — moving, many-voiced, alive — so Setnayan is taking the next step and building that new kind of memory for couples to enjoy.

## The evolution beat (the spine of every telling)

1. **Paper albums** — a few frozen frames on a shelf, turning yellow.
2. **Digital albums** — the same frozen frames, now glowing on a screen.
3. **Living memories (Setnayan)** — the day kept *alive*: moving moments, the people who couldn't be there, the stories your guests tell, all in one living page you keep forever.

## Full manifesto

> A new way to remember
>
> We have always kept the people we love in albums.
> First on paper — pages on a shelf, slowly turning yellow.
> Then on screens — the same still frames, just glowing now.
>
> **But your wedding was never still.**
>
> It was your lola laughing in the third row.
> The friend who cried during your vows.
> The cousin overseas who stayed up until 3 a.m. just to watch you say *"I do."*
> The joke you missed — because you were busy getting married.
> The story your guests are *still* telling each other — one you've never even heard.
>
> A photograph can't hold that. An album can't move.
>
> **Until now.**
>
> Setnayan keeps your wedding the way it actually happened — **alive.**
> The moments you were too busy to see, caught by the people beside you.
> The loved ones who couldn't be there, brought into the room.
> The words your guests write for you, in their own voice.
> All of it gathered into one living page — *the front-page story of your day* — that moves, that your guests help write, that you keep forever. And when you want to hold it in your hands, we print it — with a small code that always brings you back to the living version.
>
> This isn't a better album. It's **a new kind of memory.**
>
> The way we remember has been waiting for the technology to catch up.
> It just did.
>
> **Set na 'yan.**

## How the four features prove the manifesto (capture → keep)

| Line in the manifesto | The feature behind it |
|---|---|
| "the moments you were too busy to see, caught by the people beside you" | **Papic** (5 Seats + Guest) — the human moments, presence across *attention* |
| "the loved ones who couldn't be there, brought into the room" | **Live Studio** — presence across *distance* (the bedridden lola, family abroad) |
| "the words your guests write for you, in their own voice" | **Kwento** — presence across *memory* (the stories the couple never saw) |
| "one living page… that you keep forever… we print it, with a code…" | **Editorial** — the keepsake destination; "front-page story of your day" |

Note the discipline: the manifesto sells **feeling, not features.** Feature names live in the table for internal mapping only — public copy says *moments, people, stories, forever*, never "multi-camera SFU / disposable camera SKU / on-device face match."
