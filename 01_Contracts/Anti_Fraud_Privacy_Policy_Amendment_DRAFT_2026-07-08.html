Amendment 2026-07-08 — Anti-Fraud & Trust Integrity

Added 2026-07-08 to disclose how we protect the integrity of our marketplace signals (reviews, ratings, badges, booking counts) against manipulation. Status: DRAFT pending DPO (Indalecio Sacdalan Casasola II) + PH counsel review — published only after sign-off. This processing is already active; this amendment brings the public disclosure into line with it. Assessment: DPIA R-08 + RoPA DPS-12. Built to RA 10173 by design; this amendment does not replace counsel confirmation.

B.1 What we do, and why

To keep our reviews, ratings, badges, and “most-booked / top-pick” signals honest, we run automated integrity checks that detect and prevent manipulation — for example, fake or duplicate accounts created to inflate a vendor’s reputation, or coordinated fake bookings.

  • Detecting duplicate identities. To spot rings of accounts controlled by one person or household, we analyse signals we already hold: your device fingerprint, your normalised home address, and the payment-sender identity on your transactions. Accounts that share these strong signals are grouped so that duplicate reviews and bookings are counted once, not many times.
  • Scoring risk. We compute per-vendor fraud-risk signals (for example, unusual review timing, isolated single-vendor accounts, or all-5-star patterns) and combine them into a risk score.
  • Acting on it. At a high-confidence score, a vendor’s public listing may be temporarily hidden (suspended) while we review.

B.2 Lawful basis

We rely on legitimate interest (RA 10173 § 12(f)) — preventing fraud and protecting the integrity of the marketplace for couples and for honest vendors. We use only data already collected for other purposes (there is no new collection for this), applied to this compatible and now-disclosed purpose.

B.3 Automated decisions — and your right to contest them

  • Automatic suspension is reversible. When the risk score is high, a vendor’s listing may be automatically hidden and its badges frozen. No data is deleted, and a single review by our team reverses it.
  • Permanent action is never automatic. Removing a vendor’s reviews/events from our statistics and permanently banning an account requires two separate members of our team to confirm — it is a human, logged decision, never made by the system alone.
  • You can object and appeal. If you are a vendor affected by an automated suspension or an enforcement action, you may object and request a review through our Help Center or by contacting our Data Protection Officer at iscasasolaii@gmail.com. This reflects your rights under RA 10173 § 16(c) (right to object) and § 34 (rights relating to automated processing). We acknowledge and respond within the timelines in § 9 of this Policy.

B.4 What we do not do

  • We do not capture or use your IP address for this.
  • These fraud groupings, signals, and scores are strictly internal — no couple or vendor can view them. They are never sold, shared with third parties, used for advertising, or used to rank or promote vendors. They are used only for fraud prevention and marketplace integrity.
  • The evidence we store is non-identifying — counts, ratios, and internal labels, not your name, address, or raw identifiers.

B.5 Who this affects

  • Couples and guests — your device, address, and payment-sender signals may be used to detect duplicate-identity rings. You are never shown any grouping, and no automated decision is made about you from this.
  • Vendors — your public trust statistics are de-duplicated, and you may be risk-scored and (reversibly) suspended, with the human-reviewed objection and appeal path described in B.3.

B.6 Retention

Duplicate-identity groupings are recomputed on an ongoing basis and are not kept as a standing history. Fraud-risk signals and enforcement records are kept only as long as necessary for fraud prevention and to handle any related dispute or appeal, after which they are deleted or de-identified, consistent with the retention principles in § 4 of this Policy.

Contact. For any question about this processing, or to object or appeal, contact our Data Protection Officer, Indalecio Sacdalan Casasola II, at iscasasolaii@gmail.com, or use the Help Center. You may also lodge a complaint with the National Privacy Commission.
