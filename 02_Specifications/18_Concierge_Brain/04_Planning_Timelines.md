## 12-month-out checklist (the anchor window)

**Tags:** timeline, 12-month, planning, far-out, anchors, venue-first
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 1 Phase 1 + Phase 2, 0006 marketplace, 0007 budget
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

At 12 months before your wedding, lock the anchors that everything else depends on: your **ceremony venue** (church or civil registry), your **reception venue** (≤ 8km from the ceremony in our default), your **caterer** (if not bundled with the venue), your **photographer and videographer**, your **wedding coordinator**, your **florist/stylist**, and your **band or DJ**.

These are the major commitments because they're the hardest to rebook on Filipino peak dates. Popular Manila, Cebu, and Tagaytay churches book 12-24 months in advance for "Ber months" (September-December) and the April-May summer rush. Photographers with strong portfolios are usually fully booked 12+ months out for Saturday slots.

Set your **wedding date** first if you haven't already — every booking depends on this. Have a working budget in mind even if it's rough: it sizes which tier of venue, caterer, and photographer you should be browsing.

If you're getting married in less than 12 months, this whole list compresses into "do all of these in parallel, starting today." Some categories (custom bridal gowns, popular venues during peak season) may not be available — Setnayan Concierge will flag which categories are still feasible and which need a workaround.

### Common follow-ups
- "We're at 9 months — am I already too late?"
- "Which one should we book first if we're starting from scratch?"
- "How much does a Tagaytay garden venue cost for 150 guests?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't say couples *must* book at 12 months — short-engagement weddings happen often and well-planned ones still succeed; the wizard handles compressed timelines.
- Don't promise specific vendor availability — couples need to inquire directly.

## 9-10 month checklist — wedding party + supporting vendors

**Tags:** timeline, 9-month, 10-month, sponsors, hmua, bridal-car, honeymoon
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 1 Phase 2.5, 0001 guest list (role taxonomy), 0010 mood board
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

8 to 10 months before the wedding, lock your **wedding party** — your principal and secondary sponsors, plus your entourage roles. Their full names need to be confirmed before invitation design begins, since they get printed on every invitation. Book your **HMUA** (the bride's primary makeup artist, plus entourage coverage), your **bridal car**, and start **honeymoon planning** in earnest (especially if it's international — visa, passport, and visa-bridge processing can take weeks).

For Filipino couples, principal sponsors are usually the couple's chosen mentors — older married couples whose lives they look up to. Secondary sponsors handle the candle, cord, and veil during the ceremony, plus the coin ceremony. Confirm these roles early because asking is meaningful and people may decline if they have conflicts.

If you're working with multiple HMUAs (one for the bride, separate ones for entourage), this is the window to confirm them and lock dates. Same with bridal cars — the antique-classic or luxury-fleet vendors are limited inventory in NCR.

### Common follow-ups
- "How many principal sponsors do we need?"
- "Can the same HMUA do me and my bridesmaids?"
- "When should we apply for the honeymoon visa?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't insist on a fixed number of sponsors — Catholic dioceses vary, and the couple's preferences vary.
- Don't recommend specific HMUA artists — that's 0006 marketplace territory.

## 6-8 month checklist — style, design, and the mood board lock

**Tags:** timeline, 6-month, 8-month, mood-board, attire, prenup, save-the-dates
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 1 Phase 3, 0010 mood board, 0024 save-the-date
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

6 to 8 months out, the **style and design layer** comes together. This is your **bridal gown** lock window (custom designs have a 3-6 month production lead time), your **groom's suit or barong**, your **entourage attire**, your **mood board and palette lock** in Setnayan's Mood Board tool, your **save-the-dates** sent to guests, your **prenup shoot** with your photography team, your **wedding rings**, and your **cake vendor** if it's a solo booking (hotels usually bundle the cake).

**The mood board lock at this window is critical.** Your florist, stylist, cake vendor, and invitation designer all draw their designs from the colors and aesthetic you commit to in the mood board. Couples who delay the mood board create bottlenecks for everyone downstream — the florist can't propose arrangements without your palette, the cake designer can't sketch tiers without your style direction, and so on. Setnayan's Mood Board tool gives you a 6-color palette per role plus per-venue palettes (church, reception, cocktail) — lock these by month 6.

If you're choosing a custom gown, this is your hardest deadline. Most Filipino-couture designers (Cary Santiago, Francis Libiran, Mark Bumgarner, Hannah Adrias) need 3-6 months minimum and book out for peak seasons.

### Common follow-ups
- "When should I order my custom gown?"
- "Do I have to lock my palette before talking to the florist?"
- "Can my prenup photographer be different from my wedding photographer?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't recommend specific designers — that's 0006 marketplace territory.
- Don't suggest skipping the mood board — even simple weddings benefit from a locked palette for invitation + signage consistency.

## 4-5 month checklist — finalizing food, florals, and fittings

**Tags:** timeline, 4-month, 5-month, food-tasting, florals, lights, fittings
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 1 Phase 4, 0007 budget, 0006 vendor meetings
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

4 to 5 months out, you finalize the operational layer — the **food tasting and menu** with your caterer (this is non-negotiable; never sign off on a menu without tasting it), **flower arrangements designed** with your florist using your locked mood board, **lights and sound** locked, **stylist deep planning** for the venue setup, and **attire fittings begin** for the bride, groom, and entourage.

Filipino weddings traditionally include a heavy menu — multiple appetizers, several main courses, a Filipino-favorites station (lechon, kare-kare, sinigang), pasta, dessert station, and roving food carts. Use the tasting to negotiate substitutions, identify dietary restrictions, and confirm portion sizing. Don't accept "trust us, it'll be great" from a caterer who won't do a tasting.

Your florist should now have your full palette + venue diagrams. They'll propose centerpieces, ceremony arrangements, bouquets per role, and any installation pieces. Sign off on these before they order from their wholesale suppliers (which happens 6-8 weeks pre-wedding).

### Common follow-ups
- "What should I serve for cocktails?"
- "How many flower arrangements do we actually need?"
- "When do my bridesmaids need their first fitting?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't insist on a specific menu — Filipino regional preferences vary widely.
- Don't undersize lights & sound — venue acoustics matter more than couples realize.

## 3-4 month checklist — legal documents, host, and invitations

**Tags:** timeline, 3-month, 4-month, marriage-license, pre-cana, invitations, host, rsvp
**Applies to:** all couples (legal items vary by religious tradition)
**Cross-ref:** 0026 legal flows, 0028 emails, 0001 guest list, 0002 QR invitations
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

3 to 4 months before the wedding, the **legal and operational layer** locks. Apply for your **marriage license** at the Local Civil Registry (LCR) — but **not earlier than 4 months out**, because the license is only valid for 120 days. Schedule your **Pre-Cana or Marriage Encounter** sessions if you're having a Catholic wedding (parish needs 60-90 days notice). Submit your **church requirements**: CENOMAR (Certificate of No Marriage Record), baptismal certificate, confirmation certificate, and the canonical interview with the parish priest. Book your **host or emcee** for the reception. **Invitations finalized and printed**, then **invitations sent** at 2-3 months for RSVP buffer. **Begin RSVP tracking** in Setnayan's Guest List tool as responses come in.

The **marriage license is the hardest deadline** in the entire timeline. Applying earlier than 4 months means the 120-day clock will expire before your wedding date. Applying later than 3 months means you risk the LCR's 10-day publication period bumping into your timeline. Setnayan Concierge will surface this specific reminder at the right week.

For Filipino Catholic weddings, the parish's specific document requirements vary — your priest may also require seminars beyond Pre-Cana (Family Life or Couple-for-Christ). Confirm the full list with your specific parish at this stage.

### Common follow-ups
- "What if our marriage license expires before the wedding?"
- "Do we need a CENOMAR if we've never been married?"
- "How long does Pre-Cana actually take?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't apply for the marriage license too early — it has a 120-day validity from the date of issue, not application.
- Don't promise specific parish requirements — they vary diocese-to-diocese.
- Civil weddings skip the church document load entirely.

## 2-3 month checklist — invitations sent, booths, dance guide, walkthrough

**Applies to:** all couples

**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

2 to 3 months before the wedding, **invitations go out** to guests (gives Filipino guests realistic RSVP buffer — most respond in the last 2 weeks of the window), book any remaining **photobooth or cocktail-hour vendors** (coffee bar, perfume bar, mobile bar), book a **dance choreographer** for the first dance and grand march if you want one, do your **final venue walkthrough** with your coordinator, and start your **seating chart draft** in Setnayan's Seating tool.

Filipino RSVP culture trends late — most guests reply in the final two weeks of the invitation period, often confirming via Messenger or phone call instead of through formal channels. Use Setnayan's QR-based digital RSVP to capture both formal and informal responses; nudge unresponsive guests 2 weeks before the wedding via the Guest List nudge feature.

Your final venue walkthrough should include your coordinator, your stylist, and ideally your photographer. Run through the day's flow: ceremony arrival, photo locations, reception entrance, table layout, dance floor placement.

### Common follow-ups
- "What if guests RSVP late?"
- "Do we need a photobooth?"
- "When should I finalize the seating chart?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say

## 1-month checklist — final headcount, day-of timeline, balance payments

**Tags:** timeline, 1-month, headcount, day-of-timeline, balance-payments, rehearsal
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0007 budget § payment milestones, 0028 emails
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

1 month before the wedding, lock the **final guest headcount** with your caterer — this drives the final billing and how many crew meals are needed. **Day-of timeline** is finalized and distributed to every vendor (ceremony arrival, photo blocks, processional, reception entrance, speeches, dances, send-off). **Rehearsal dinner planned** if you're hosting one (usually the night before). **Vendor balance payments** are scheduled (most vendors expect 70-80% paid by T-30 days, balance day-of or post-wedding). Beauty appointments scheduled (hair trial, manicure, brows). Cash envelopes prepared for vendors not on Setnayan Pay — Filipino wedding-day vendor tips are typically cash, given by the coordinator on behalf of the couple.

The final headcount is your last major financial decision. Caterers price by per-pax (per-plate or per-buffet-cover), and most contracts have a 5-10% adjustment window from your initial quote. Going above means paying overages; going below means the caterer keeps the difference but you can't recover it. Aim for accuracy.

### Common follow-ups
- "What if more guests show up than RSVP'd?"
- "How much cash do I need for vendor tips?"
- "Do I need a separate day-of coordinator?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't lock the headcount too early — wait for the 2-week RSVP window to close.
- Don't recommend specific tip amounts — they're personal.

## Day-of timeline — the typical Filipino wedding day

**Tags:** day-of, schedule, event-day, ceremony, reception, send-off
**Applies to:** all couples (varies by religious tradition)
**Cross-ref:** 0031 day-of guest experience, 0037 event-day preload
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

A typical Filipino Catholic wedding day runs from early morning prep through late-evening send-off. Sample timeline (3pm ceremony, 6pm reception):

- **6:00 AM** — Bride begins hair & makeup (HMUA arrives, breakfast for bride + entourage)
- **8:00 AM** — Groom prep begins
- **10:00 AM** — Photo team begins coverage at bride's home
- **12:00 PM** — Bride transitions to ceremony venue (changing room at church)
- **2:00 PM** — Ceremony rehearsal walkthrough with sponsors
- **3:00 PM** — Ceremony begins (procession · vows · communion · sponsorship rites · candle/cord/veil · arras · presentation as Mr. & Mrs.)
- **4:00 PM** — Family photos at church
- **4:45 PM** — Travel to reception venue (couple separately for "grand entrance" prep)
- **6:00 PM** — Reception begins (cocktail hour with photo booth · live music)
- **7:00 PM** — Grand entrance · first dance · parents' dances · welcome remarks
- **7:30 PM** — Dinner served
- **8:30 PM** — Speeches (parents, sponsors, best man, maid of honor · usually 4-6 total)
- **9:15 PM** — Cake cutting · money dance · garter & bouquet toss
- **10:00 PM** — Open dance floor · party
- **11:00 PM** — Send-off (sparklers, bubbles, or rice toss)

Adjust for ceremony tradition (Civil ceremonies are shorter; Muslim weddings have different ritual blocks; combined or non-traditional flows vary widely).

### Common follow-ups
- "When should we cut the cake?"
- "How long should speeches be?"
- "Do we still do garter and bouquet toss?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't prescribe ritual order — Filipino weddings vary by family preference, region, and religious tradition.
- Don't make timing promises — actual day-of runs always over schedule by 15-30 minutes.

## What to book FIRST (dependency-driven ordering)

**Tags:** book-first, dependencies, order, priority, locked-sequence
**Applies to:** all couples
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 1 The Locked Sequence
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

The reason Setnayan recommends a specific booking order is that vendor decisions cascade: **venue locks date → date locks officiant → officiant scope locks photographer's flow → caterer headcount locks invitations → invitations lock RSVP → RSVP locks seating**. Skip a step and downstream vendors can't quote you accurately.

Book in this order for the smoothest planning:

1. **Ceremony venue** (church or civil registry) — locks your date most rigidly
2. **Reception venue** — needs to be ≤ 8km from ceremony for guest comfort
3. **Caterer** — drives per-head budget math for everything else
4. **Photographer + videographer** — peak-date Saturdays book 12+ months out
5. **Wedding coordinator** — they orchestrate everything downstream
6. **Florist/stylist** — their proposals depend on your mood board
7. **Band/DJ/host** — entertainment programming
8. **HMUA** — locks your day-of beauty timeline
9. **Bridal car** — fleet inventory is limited
10. **Bridal gown + groom suit** — custom designs need 3-6 month lead

After these are locked, the operational decisions (invitations, seating, photobooth, dance choreographer, lights/sound details, attire fittings, cake design, save-the-dates) flow from them in the natural order surfaced by the Concierge wizard.

### Common follow-ups
- "What if I find a photographer before a venue?"
- "Can we skip the coordinator?"
- "Why does the order matter so much?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't insist on rigid ordering — couples may have specific vendors they're already committed to.
- Don't recommend skipping the coordinator for full-service weddings >100 guests — operational complexity overwhelms most couples.

## Marriage license timeline (the 120-day hard window)

**Tags:** marriage-license, lcr, civil, paperwork, 120-day-validity, publication-period
**Applies to:** all couples (Civil + Religious)
**Cross-ref:** 0026 legal flows, 0028 emails
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

Your marriage license has a **120-day validity** from the day it's issued. This makes it the hardest deadline in your entire planning timeline. Apply too early and the license expires before your wedding. Apply too late and you risk the LCR's 10-day publication period bumping into your wedding date.

The optimal application window is **4 months before the wedding**. Walk into the Local Civil Registry of either party's city or municipality (you can choose) with these documents:

- Birth certificate (PSA-issued, original)
- CENOMAR — Certificate of No Marriage Record (PSA-issued)
- Valid government ID for both parties
- Community Tax Certificate (cedula)
- Parental consent (if either party is 18-21) or parental advice (22-25)
- 4 1x1 photos each
- Filing fee (varies by LGU, typically ₱100-500)

After application, the LCR posts a 10-day public notice (the "10-day publication period"). After that, the license is issued — and the 120-day clock starts on issuance, not application.

Setnayan Concierge surfaces this specific timing reminder at the right week to make sure you apply at the optimal moment.

### Common follow-ups
- "What if our marriage license expires before the wedding?"
- "Can we apply in a different city than where we live?"
- "What's a CENOMAR?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't tell couples to apply earlier than 4 months — they'll lose the license.
- Don't promise specific LCR fees — they vary by city.
- Don't recommend skipping the license — it's legally required.

## Catholic Pre-Cana / Marriage Encounter timeline

**Tags:** pre-cana, marriage-encounter, catholic, requirements, parish, seminar
**Applies to:** Catholic couples
**Cross-ref:** 0026 legal flows, 01_Filipino_Cultural_Reference.md
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

Catholic Filipino couples are typically required to attend a **Pre-Cana seminar** before the wedding. Parishes vary in their specific requirements, but most need **60-90 days notice** to schedule you into their Pre-Cana batch.

Common parish requirements for Catholic weddings:

- **Pre-Cana seminar** (or Marriage Encounter Weekend) — usually 1-2 days
- **Family Life / Couple-for-Christ seminars** — some parishes require these as additional
- **Canonical interview** with the parish priest — separate appointment, 30-60 minutes
- **Document submission**: baptismal certificate (PSA-issued, within 6 months), confirmation certificate (PSA-issued, within 6 months), CENOMAR
- **Banns of marriage** — public posting of the marriage announcement at both parties' parishes for 3 consecutive Sundays (some dioceses)

**Schedule your Pre-Cana at 4-5 months out** — many parishes only run Pre-Cana monthly or bimonthly, so leaving it later than 4 months may force you into a slot that conflicts with other planning.

Setnayan Concierge surfaces a reminder at the 5-month mark specifically for Catholic couples. If you're combining Catholic + Civil ceremonies, both sets of requirements stack — start the Pre-Cana track 6 months out for safety.

### Common follow-ups
- "Do we have to do Pre-Cana?"
- "Which parish should we use?"
- "What if our parish requires extra seminars?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't promise the same requirements across all dioceses — they vary significantly.
- Don't recommend skipping Pre-Cana — Catholic couples typically can't get married without it.

## What can wait vs what's urgent (priority triage)

**Tags:** urgent, can-wait, priority, planning, compressed-timeline
**Applies to:** all couples (especially short-engagement)
**Cross-ref:** 0016 § 3 importance tiers
**Last verified:** 2026-05-18 · Setnayan team

If you're working on a compressed timeline (< 6 months) or feel overwhelmed by the full list, here's how to triage:

**Urgent (lock these now):**
- Ceremony venue + date
- Reception venue
- Photographer + videographer
- Caterer
- Wedding coordinator (especially if short timeline — they'll catch what you miss)
- Marriage license (if within 4 months of wedding)

**Important but can flex (book in next 2 months):**
- Mood board lock — drives downstream vendors
- Bridal gown (custom = book NOW; off-the-rack = can wait to 3 months)
- Sponsors confirmed — needed before invitations
- Florist, HMUA, host, band/DJ

**Can wait until 2-3 months out:**
- Photobooth + cocktail booths
- Dance choreographer
- Final invitations design
- Seating chart draft

**Don't worry about until 1 month out:**
- Final headcount lock
- Day-of timeline distribution
- Cash envelope prep
- Beauty appointments
- Final fittings

**Common mistakes that derail couples:**
- Deciding the cake design before the caterer confirms (cake should reflect catering style)
- Finalizing seating before RSVP cutoff (people change, you'll rework it)
- Booking florals before locking the mood board (florist needs your palette)
- Sending invitations before sponsors confirm (their names go on the invitations)
- Applying for the marriage license earlier than 4 months out (it expires)

The Setnayan Concierge wizard handles this triage automatically — it surfaces what's urgent right now, what's coming up next week, and what can safely wait. If your wedding is closer than the standard windows, the wizard will flag impossible-in-time items (e.g., "custom gown won't be ready") so you can pivot to alternatives.

### Common follow-ups
- "We're getting married in 4 months — what should we do FIRST?"
- "What if my ideal vendor is fully booked?"
- "Can we skip Pre-Cana with a 3-month engagement?"

### Caveats / what NOT to say
- Don't be alarmist about short engagements — many beautiful weddings happen in 3-6 months.
- Don't tell couples to "just do it all in parallel" — overwhelm leads to mistakes.
- Don't promise specific vendor availability — couples must inquire directly.
